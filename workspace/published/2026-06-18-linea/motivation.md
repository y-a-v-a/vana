# Linea

Between 1959 and 1960 Piero Manzoni drew lines on long rolls of paper, sealed each roll inside a labelled cardboard or lead tube, and forbade its opening. The label stated the line's exact length — *Linea m 4,77*, *Linea m 7,200* — and that statement was all anyone would ever have. The line was real, complete, and permanently out of view. Value migrated entirely onto the certificate; to look would be to destroy the work. **Linea** restages this on the web, where the gesture is no longer a conceit but a literal fact of the medium.

On each visit the page picks a length the way Manzoni did — anything from thirty centimetres to several kilometres, log-uniform so the absurd lengths show up — and then *genuinely draws the line*: a random walk of up to 2,400 vertices, normalised so its summed segment length equals the declared metres. The geometry exists in memory, full-length, complete. It is simply never written to a visible surface. The withholding is not a metaphor; it is `drawAndSeal()` returning without ever touching the DOM or a canvas. The connoisseur who "opens the tube" by opening the developer console finds the line *attested* — length, vertex count, date — but still never *illustrated*. That is Duchamp's infrathin: the immeasurable difference between a line that exists-but-is-unseen and no line at all.

This enacts **P1** (concept *is* execution: the line is truly made and truly sealed by the same function), **P2** (the digital readymade — the measurement and the dated label *are* the certificate), **P3** (the smallest build that completes the thought: one tube, one number, one withheld path), **P6** (value and authenticity staged literally — the sealed tube is priceless precisely because it cannot be inspected), and **P7** (double-coded: a sleek sealed tube for the casual viewer, a precise note on dematerialised conceptual art for the literate one). It sits on the **P5** chance/order axis — a chance-driven random walk constrained to an exact, ordered measurement.

The mechanism is the argument. A print of a sealed tube would merely *depict* a withheld line; here the line is computed in front of you and then refused, on a medium whose default verb is *render*. Each "Seal another line" discards the last forever — only change, nothing kept (**P4**), no storage, no network, no cookies. The work answers Manzoni's tubes the only way the web can: by being honest enough to make the thing and disciplined enough never to show it.
