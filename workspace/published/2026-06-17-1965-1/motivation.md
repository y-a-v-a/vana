# 1965 / 1 – ∞

Roman Opałka spent forty-six years painting the integers from 1 toward infinity, one canvas after another, beginning each *Détail* where the last one stopped. From 1968 he added one percent white to the ground every year, so that the figures grew paler until they were white-on-white — *blanc mérité*, the earned white. He spoke each number aloud onto tape and photographed his own face after every session. The count was one continuous, irreversible line, and his life was the price of admission.

This page restages that line as a web behaviour. On load the count begins — not at his number, but at **1**, because the browser remembers nothing — and rises forever via the render loop. As the figure climbs it is interpolated toward the colour of its own ground, an asymptote it never quite reaches: you watch the number bleach itself into *blanc mérité* in seconds, then reload and it is born again at 1. **The amnesia is the work.** Opałka had one life and no undo; the web offers infinity for free and forgets it instantly.

It enacts **P1** (concept *is* execution: the bleach is not a style applied to the number — the same rising count both drives the count and computes its disappearance, so the figure literally erases itself by existing) and **P5** on the *order* pole (a strict monotonic rule, no chance, no input — Opałka's discipline as an algorithm). It carries **P6** quietly: the wall label, priced in time rather than dollars, notes that "each reload begins again at 1; he had only the one life" — value located in irreversibility, the one thing a stateless page cannot have. **P4** (live, regenerating, never fixed) and **P3** (the smallest build that completes the thought: one number, one tint function) hold the rest.

The reference it answers is **Roman Opałka, *OPAŁKA 1965 / 1 – ∞* (the *Détails*, 1965–2011)**, and behind him the conceptual lineage of counting as a life-measure (On Kawara's *I Am Still Alive*). It is double-coded (**P7**): a casual visitor sees a number quietly fading on an off-white screen; a literate one sees *blanc mérité* reproduced as an interpolation curve and the cheerful-cynic point that digital infinity costs nothing and means nothing, because it cannot be lost.

Mechanism, not decoration: nothing is fetched, stored, or tracked. The count exists only while the tab renders, and the bleaching, the resetting, and the asymptote are all the same arithmetic — the medium's free, amnesiac, monotonic counting *is* the elegy for a man who made the same count cost everything.
