# Measurement: Viewport

In 1969 Mel Bochner stopped putting objects in the room and measured the room instead — drawing dimension lines on the gallery walls and writing the distances next to them. The work was the space's own measurement, made visible. The wall did not hold the art; the wall *was* the art, once stated in inches. **Measurement: Viewport** moves that gesture onto the web's only wall: the browser viewport. The page contains nothing but the live measurement of the space it is shown in — width across the top, height down the left, with a draftsman's footnote on the device-pixel ratio and the total area.

The argument is in the mechanism, not in any image (P1, G3). The figures are not typed in; they are read from `window.innerWidth` / `innerHeight` and redrawn on every `resize`. So the work cannot exist as a print — it has no fixed size to print (G1). Bochner's room had one set of dimensions; the web has none. Every visitor's window is a differently sized gallery, and dragging the window edge re-measures the piece in real time. The smallest build that completes the thought (P3): two arrowed dimension lines and the numbers they report, nothing hung between them.

This sits deliberately on the **order** end of the chance/order axis (P5) — strict measurement, ruled lines, a single legible system — while chance enters only through the container the visitor happens to bring: their screen, their window, their `devicePixelRatio`. The double coding (P7) is direct: a casual viewer gets the dry gag — *the artwork is just the size of your window* — while a literate viewer reads the conceptual-art claim that the space is the work, now sharpened by the fact that the web has abolished the fixed dimension Bochner could still nail to a wall.

It answers Mel Bochner's *Measurement: Room* (1969) and the *Measurement Series* (1968–69), and stands in the net.art tradition of treating browser chrome and viewport as material rather than frame. Ethics are part of the piece (P8): Creative Commons BY-SA 4.0, attribution to y-a-v-a and to Bochner, no cookies, no trackers, nothing stored — the measurement is taken, shown, and forgotten, freshly per visit (P4). It is materially distinct from the catalogue's responsive Magritte (which *stretches* an image) and from *this way browser* (which sums pointer travel into physical distance): here nothing moves and nothing is drawn but the dimensions of the empty space itself.
