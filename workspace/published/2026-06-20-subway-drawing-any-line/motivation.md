# Subway Drawing (any line)

In the 1960s William Anastasi sat on the New York subway, paper on his knees, a pencil in each hand, eyes closed, and let the train draw. The marks are a recording of the car's motion through his idle body — automatism handed not to the unconscious but to the transit authority. He called the related practice *Pocket Drawings*: paper carried, scribbled by walking. The artist's only decision is to stop deciding.

This work makes the browser do exactly that. The `DeviceMotionEvent` accelerometer — a sensor that exists only because the page lives on a device that can be carried — becomes the pencil. A high-pass filter strips away steady gravity and keeps the jolts; each jolt displaces the pen and lays down a segment of graphite line. You are told to pocket the phone and not look. The train, the bus, the walk, the nervous hand on a café table: these draw, you do not. This is **P1** (the mechanism *is* the concept — the sensor is literally the surrendered hand, not a metaphor for it) and **P5 chance** (the line's form is determined by involuntary motion, the cheerful-cynic end of the chance/order axis where automatism meets a gyroscope).

It is a **digital readymade** (**P2**): the phone's motion sensor, built to rotate maps and count steps, is recontextualised as a seismograph for mark-making. The novelty is one of location and found-object — only the web, running on a sensor in a moving pocket, can make this drawing; printed, it would be a dead squiggle, and on a perfectly still desk it honestly admits "the train has stopped." Nothing is stored or sent (no network, no cookies, **P8**); reload erases the ride, so the work is a behaviour and not an artifact (**P4**). The data plate reports acceleration and mark count with deadpan restraint (**P9**) — the only signature is the world's own trembling.

It answers Anastasi directly, and behind him Surrealist automatic drawing and the long question of who authors a mark when the artist refuses to. The twist that keeps it out of reverent-homage territory: the sitter is no longer the artist on a train but *you*, holding a tracker you were told not to watch, and the work conspicuously refuses to track you back.
