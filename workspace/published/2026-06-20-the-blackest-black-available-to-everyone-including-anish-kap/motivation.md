# The Blackest Black (available to everyone, including Anish Kapoor)

In 2016 the artist Anish Kapoor acquired the exclusive artistic licence to Vantablack — at the time the blackest black ever made — and no other artist could use it. Stuart Semple answered by selling his own ultra-blacks (and his "pinkest pink") to *everyone except Anish Kapoor*: to buy them you ticked a box swearing you were not him. This work answers that quarrel from the one place where the whole fight is moot — the screen. A screen's blackest black is simply every subpixel switched off: it costs nothing, renders identically on every device, and can be owned by no one. The page is that maximal black, presented with a museum wall label, and it hands the colour back to the viewer.

The mechanism *is* the argument (**P1**). Clicking the field does not depict generosity, it performs it: a local `navigator.clipboard.writeText('#000000')` transfers the work's entire material — its colour — directly into your possession, with no network, no server, no token, nothing withheld. The open licence is not a footnote bolted onto an image; the act of giving the colour away **is** the artwork (**P8** — the delivery ethics are the content). Each gift leaves no less black behind; the session counter notes it has been given away N times "and there is no less of it," staging scarcity's collapse the moment the medium is light instead of pigment.

It is a digital readymade of a market scandal (**P2**) aimed squarely at value, ownership and authenticity (**P6**): the absurdity of *owning a colour* is exposed by a medium that cannot enforce the claim. Stuart Semple excluded Kapoor; the sharper rebuke is to include him — this black is released to everyone, "Kapoor included." The double-coding (**P7**, **P9**) lets a casual visitor get the dry gag of a black page that says "it's yours," while the literate viewer reads the Vantablack/Semple feud, the lineage of the monochrome (Malevich's *Black Square*, Reinhardt's black paintings), and net.art's instinct that anything reproducible at zero cost resists the market by default. Built as the smallest thing that completes the thought (**P3**): a black field, a label, one click.

It is live rather than fixed (**P4**): the black is whatever maximum *your* panel can produce, re-rendered per device, and the transfer happens in real time in your hands. A photograph of a black painting could hang on a wall; this could not — only the web can give the same colour to everyone at once, including the man who tried to buy it.

**References answered:** Anish Kapoor / Vantablack exclusive licence (2016); Stuart Semple, *Black 2.0 / 3.0* and *the Pinkest Pink* ("available to everyone except Anish Kapoor"); in lineage, Kazimir Malevich's *Black Square* (1915) and Ad Reinhardt's black paintings; net.art's economy of the costless copy.

**Distinct from the catalogue:** unlike *Abstract Painting (Backlit)* — which is about reproduction quantizing tinted blacks to nothing — this work's source (the Kapoor/Semple ownership feud), mechanism (a clipboard grant releasing the colour as an open licence) and argument (you cannot own #000000 on a screen) are materially different (G6).
