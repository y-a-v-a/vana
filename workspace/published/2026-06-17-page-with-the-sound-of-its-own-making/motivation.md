# Page with the Sound of Its Own Making

In 1961 Robert Morris sealed a walnut cube around a three-and-a-half-hour tape recording of the sawing and hammering that built it. *Box with the Sound of Its Own Making* is the founding gesture of process art: the object and the account of its becoming are the same thing, and you cannot have one without enduring the other. This page is that box, relocated to the runtime. On **make**, it reads its own live source — `document.documentElement.outerHTML`, the very text rendering in front of you — and plays it one character at a time: each byte a square-wave note, each space and newline a rest. The "making" of a webpage is no longer a studio with hand tools; it is a browser parsing source. So the page performs the only making it has: itself, out loud.

The mechanism is the argument (**P1**). I am not illustrating Morris with a decorative soundtrack; the audio is a deterministic function of the source code, and the source code includes the script that produces the audio. The box therefore contains the sound of its own making in the strict, recursive sense — you are hearing the loop that is doing the hearing. Nothing is recorded, because nothing was ever *made then played back*; the making happens now, in the AudioContext, the moment you ask for it (**P4** — live, per-visit, never fixed). It is a **digital readymade** (**P2**): a canonical sculpture re-staged through a browser-native behaviour rather than reproduced as an image.

It sits toward the **order** pole of the chance/order axis (**P5**): a fixed source mapped by a fixed rule to a fixed melody — yet the "composition" was written by no one, only assembled by the file, so the tune is found, not authored. Double-coded (**P7**): the casual visitor gets a black box that hums its own guts and scrolls a yellow cursor through code; the literate visitor hears the whole minimalist-into-conceptual lineage — Morris, the dematerialised object, "the process is the work" — updated for an art that ships as text and executes as sound. The wit is dry and cheerful-cynic (**P9**): a page so self-absorbed it will sing you its boilerplate, whitespace and all, and call the result a sculpture.

Smallest build that completes the thought (**P3**): one page, one play button, Web Audio, no assets, no network, no storage. It would die instantly as a print — a print cannot read itself, and a recording would betray the whole point by being *made earlier, elsewhere*. Distinct from the catalogue: where *The Original* (2026) lets you **download** the page's source as a fake edition, this one **performs** the source as a process; where *The webpage going on and off* borrows Creed's timing, this borrows Morris's recursion. Reference answered: **Robert Morris, *Box with the Sound of Its Own Making* (1961)**; web-native lineage: net.art and the page-as-its-own-medium.
