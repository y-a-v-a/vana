Optical Mixture

after Georges Seurat — pixels on screen, undated

Medium
broken colour; mixing performed by the viewer's retina
Viewing distance
—

The colours are not mixed on the screen. They are mixed in your eye, at a distance. Step back, or squint. Whatever you do, do not zoom in.

A field of complementary dots — orange and blue — that average into a luminous neutral only when small and adjacent. Seurat let the eye do the mixing, on the assumption of a fixed viewing distance. The browser grants infinite, free zoom, so that assumption is a contract the viewer can void at any time: the closer you look, the further the dots drift apart, and the picture you came for stops existing.
