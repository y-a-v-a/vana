手卷

A handscroll is meant to be unrolled between two hands, one arm’s length at a time, and read from right to left — then rolled away. It was never hung on a wall. It was never meant to be seen whole.

A museum lays the whole thing flat behind glass, lit end to end, and calls that respect. Your browser refuses: it can only ever hold open one length at a time. You have just done the one thing the vitrine forbids — you rolled it.

After Wang Ximeng, A Thousand Li of Rivers and Mountains (千里江山圖), 1113 — a scroll so fragile that unrolling it is itself the reason it is almost never shown.
