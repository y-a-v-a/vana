# An Artist Who Cannot Speak English Is No Artist

In 1992 Mladen Stilinović hung a pink banner painted with the deadpan sentence *An Artist Who Cannot Speak English Is No Artist* — a flatly stated indictment of the international art world's quiet rule that legitimacy, exhibitions and careers flow in one language. This work restages that banner for the browser and then does the unforgivable thing the banner only implies: it actually runs the test on you. The page reads `navigator.languages` — the ordered list of languages *you* configured in *your* browser — and issues a verdict. English in the list: "You are an artist." English absent: "You are no artist." Each visitor receives a different ruling, computed live, on their own machine. A print of the banner accuses the room; this version accuses the reader by name (G1, P4).

The mechanism *is* the argument (P1, G3). Art-world legitimacy — the whole apparatus of who gets called an artist — is here reduced to a single boolean string check on a browser preference. Nothing in the page evaluates the work, the idea, the craft; it evaluates a locale tag. And because `navigator.languages` is a setting and not a fact about you, the closing line is literal, not rhetorical: *to appeal, add English and reload.* You can promote yourself from "no artist" to "artist" by editing a dropdown. The arbitrariness Stilinović pointed at is not described; it is executed and then handed to you as a config option (P6 — power, conventions and value; the DNA's "game where power, conventions and art come together").

It is a digital readymade (P2): a known, named cultural object pulled through a web-native behavior — the same Duchampian move that runs through the catalogue, here applied to a conceptual banner rather than a urinal. It reads at two depths (P7): the casual visitor gets a dry gag about a webpage presuming to certify their artisthood; the literate visitor gets Stilinović's critique of English hegemony enacted by the very gatekeeping infrastructure of the global internet. The humor carries the idea rather than replacing it (P9) — both verdicts are hollow on purpose. The "artist" is told a certificate is unavailable and there is nothing to frame; the "no artist" is told the logic isn't the management's. Nobody wins; the gate itself is the punchline.

The reference it answers is Stilinović directly, in the lineage of Duchamp's readymade and the net.art tradition of treating browser chrome and APIs as material. Ethically it holds the floor (G4, P8): `navigator.languages` is read and displayed only in the viewer's own browser, sent nowhere, stored nowhere — no cookies, no network, no server. Attribution to Stilinović and y-a-v-a is visible and permanent regardless of the verdict; the open CC BY-SA license is stated on the page. It is the smallest build that completes the thought (P3): one sentence, one string check, one ruling.
