# Vanitas (Charge Remaining)

A single candle burns in the dark. Its flame is not decoration and not a timer — it is a live reading of `navigator.getBattery().level`. The height, brightness and steadiness of the flame *are* your device's remaining charge. Unplug your laptop and the flame slowly sinks; watch long enough and it gutters, smokes, and is nearly snuffed. Put it on the mains and it stands tall again: death deferred, not defeated. The Dutch still-life painters put a guttering candle beside the skull and the hourglass to say *your time is running out.* Here the candle finally tells the literal truth: it is measuring the mortality of the machine you are reading it on.

The mechanism is the argument (P1). A vanitas is a picture *about* transience; this one is *powered by* the transience it depicts. Concept and execution are the same object — there is no "idea" separable from the API call. It is a digital readymade (P2): the Battery Status API, a piece of mundane platform plumbing, is recontextualised as the wick of a memento mori. It lives only as behaviour (P4) — a per-device, per-moment state that no screenshot preserves — and it sits on the chance/order axis (P5): a deterministic hardware reading feeds a stochastic, guttering flame.

There is a second, sharper coding for the literate viewer (P7). Firefox and Safari **removed** the Battery Status API years ago, because advertisers were using battery level and charge-time as a fingerprint to track people across sites. So on the privacy-respecting browsers, the candle cannot read your device's life at all — the readout says so plainly, and the flame flickers on as an unquantified, borrowed light. A vanitas denied its own memento mori by the same anti-surveillance conscience this practice already lives by (P8: CC licence, no cookies, no trackers, nothing leaves the page). The one API honest enough to tell you you are dying was retired for being too good at watching you.

It answers the Dutch **vanitas** tradition directly — Harmen Steenwyck's and Pieter Claesz's candles, skulls and hourglasses — and stands apart from the catalogue's other mortality pieces: *Perfect Lovers (Reset)* used an *invented* battery-depletion **model** of two clocks; this uses the **real** hardware API and a wholly different reference. The voice is the cheerful cynic (P9): the joke — a website that dies when your phone does — carries a real point about power, dependence, and the surveillance economy that quietly reads your battery. Smallest build that completes the thought (P3): one canvas, one candle, one API, no libraries, no requests.
