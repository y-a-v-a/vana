# I Am Still Alive (Caret)

A black screen holds one thing: a single, tall text caret, blinking. That blink is
not an animation the page wrote. It is drawn by your operating system, at an interval
the page cannot set, in a rhythm the page cannot read. There is no web API for the
caret's blink rate and no event that fires when it turns on or off. So the site does
the only two honest things left to it: it opens an editable field, colours the caret,
and steps back. It hosts a pulse it does not own.

The field is kept eternally empty. Every keystroke, paste and drop is refused, so the
writing surface never becomes writing — it only performs being alive. This answers
**On Kawara**'s *I Am Still Alive* telegrams (1970– ), in which the message was reduced
to the bare fact of a heartbeat sent to someone, dated, then repeated. Here the same
telegram is transmitted once per blink, forever, to no one, and the artist is not even
allowed to time it. The uncontrollable, unreadable gap between blink-on and blink-off
is **Duchamp's** *infrathin* made literal.

The mechanism **is** the argument (**P1**): the artwork is not a picture of a pulse, it
is a real UI primitive the page is structurally forbidden to author, measure, or keep
running. That is also its novelty of ontology — a work whose only living element is a
found piece of browser/OS chrome (**P2**, the digital readymade), impossible as a print
and impossible to screenshot without killing it (the still frame is a corpse). It is
the smallest build that completes the thought (**P3**): almost no code, and every line
of it works to make the page do less. It is a behaviour, not an artifact (**P4**).

It is double-coded (**P7**): a casual viewer sees a big, faintly hypnotic cursor
"waiting for input" — the web's universal sign of a blank page about to be filled — and
smiles at a document that will never be filled; a literate viewer reads Kawara's
durational vigil and the infrathin. And it keeps the cheerful-cynic voice (**P9**): the
piece stops the instant you click away or switch tabs — the pulse flatlines, a grey
line offers *the pulse has stopped — click anywhere*, and the page admits it cannot
compel your attention back. Under some system accessibility settings the caret may not
blink at all; the page can't tell, and says so. It is alive only for as long as you
choose to keep looking, and even then, on someone else's clock.
