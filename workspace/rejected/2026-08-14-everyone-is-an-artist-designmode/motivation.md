# Everyone Is an Artist (designMode)

**Concept.** Joseph Beuys' most-quoted claim — *"Jeder Mensch ist ein Künstler"* (Everyone is an artist), the seed of his idea of *Soziale Plastik* / social sculpture — is not illustrated here, it is granted. A single line, `document.designMode = 'on'`, turns the whole document editable by whoever is looking at it. The wall label, the licence, the manifesto, and the artist's signature are all inside the editable surface, so the viewer can rewrite the work, correct it, cross it out, sign over "y-a-v-a", or delete the piece entirely. The author keeps no privileged, protected word.

**Principles enacted.** P1 (concept *is* execution): to assert that everyone is an artist, the page hands everyone the browser's own authorship tool over *everything*, author included — the mechanism is the claim, not a picture of it. P2 (digital readymade): `designMode` is an off-the-shelf browser capability, appropriated whole. P3 (minimal gesture, maximal idea): the entire build is one property assignment and no event handlers — nothing listens, reacts, or records. P4 (live, not fixed): the work only exists in the act of editing and is different in every session. P7 (double-coding): the surface gag — "wait, I can type in the *title* and in the artist's *signature*" — lands instantly without wall text, while the literate reading is Beuys' social sculpture crossed with the anti-monument, anti-surveillance ethic. P8/P9: open share-alike licence, zero persistence, and a dry cheerful-cynic voice ("an eternal monument with the memory of a goldfish").

**References it answers.** Directly, Joseph Beuys — *Jeder Mensch ist ein Künstler* and *Soziale Plastik*. Within the catalogue it extends **The Death of the Author (2026)**, but inverts its scope and its point: that work strikes one byline in a `contenteditable` article to stage Barthes' reader-is-born; this one uses whole-document `designMode` with *no* scripted reaction, so authority is not transferred from author to reader — it is dissolved for everyone at once, then forgotten on reload.

**Why the mechanism is the argument, not decoration.** The refusal to persist is the thesis, not a limitation. Because there is no storage, no cookie, and no network, no contribution can be owned, monetised, or monumentalised — Beuys' collective sculpture made literal *and* honest: infinitely participatory, infinitely equal, and gone the instant you leave. Deletion being permitted (you can erase the artwork with the Backspace key) is the same argument from the other side: if everyone is an artist, un-making is also a legitimate gesture. Nothing about this could survive as a print; the print is exactly the thing it refuses to be.
