# Today

On Kawara painted the same subject for forty-seven years: today's date, by hand, in the language and convention of whatever country he was in, in muted monochrome. The rules were strict — if a painting was not finished by midnight of the day it named, it was destroyed. The survivors were boxed in cardboard lined with that day's local newspaper. *Today* re-stages this as a webpage that paints **your** today, from your device's clock and your locale, then enacts his destruction rule literally: at local midnight the date changes, the painting is repainted, and the old one is gone. Nothing is stored — no box, no newspaper, no record, no `localStorage`, no cookie, no request of any kind.

This is a **digital readymade** (P2) and a case of **concept *is* execution** (P1): the work is not a *picture* of a date painting, it is the date, served. The `Intl.DateTimeFormat` locale is "the country he was in"; the visitor's clock is the studio day; the `setInterval` ticking toward midnight is the destruction rule, not an illustration of it. It is **live, not fixed** (P4) — regenerated per visit, never the same painting twice across timezones, and self-erasing every twenty-four hours. It sits on the **order** end of the chance/order axis (P5): a single strict rule set, encoded, running forever. And it carries a dry argument about value and labour (P6, P9): On Kawara gave a full day — up to eight hours of disciplined attention — to each canvas; the browser reports it "Painted in 3 ms," then throws it away by design.

The reference it answers is named on the page: **On Kawara, *Today* (Date Paintings), 1966–2013**. One deep-cut honours the source for the literate viewer (P7): when the visitor's locale uses a non-Roman script, the date falls back to **Esperanto**, exactly as On Kawara did when he could not date a painting in the local language. The casual viewer reads a clean, monochrome date painting that happens to be theirs; the literate viewer reads a meditation on time, labour, mechanical reproduction, and the impossibility of keeping anything on a medium whose only constant is change.

The mechanism is the message. A print of a date painting is a relic — a kept thing, the exact category On Kawara's destruction rule fights. Only the web can paint today on demand, in your tongue, on your clock, and refuse to keep it. Smallest build that completes the thought (P3): one ground colour chosen by the date, one line of text, one countdown, no persistence.
