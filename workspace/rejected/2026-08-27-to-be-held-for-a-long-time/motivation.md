# To Be Held for a Long Time

La Monte Young's *Composition 1960 #7* is a whole score: a staff bearing two notes — B and F♯, a perfect fifth — and the instruction "to be held for a long time." It is an idea that only exists as duration. This page realises it the only honest way a machine can: two Web Audio `OscillatorNode`s, a single gain, and a `.start()` with no `.stop()` ever scheduled. The browser's audio render loop then does exactly what the score demands, literally and indefinitely — the tone is held for as long as the tab lives. The instruction *is* the program.

**Mechanism as argument (P1, P4).** The concept and the execution are the same object. "To be held for a long time" is not illustrated here; it is compiled. A duration cannot be printed, framed, or reproduced as an artifact — it can only be *performed*, live, which is precisely what the audio loop does and a static image cannot (G1). The only element that changes is a clock reading `AudioContext.currentTime` — a wink at the y-a-v-a manifesto ("there is no art piece, only change") set against a tone that is pure stasis. And the web's ordinary affordance — a stop button — is deliberately withheld, because the score gives none; you end the piece the way you leave Young & Zazeela's *Dream House*: by leaving the room (closing or muting the tab). Withholding the expected control is the gesture.

**Order, and the just fifth (P5, P7).** This sits at the extreme *order* pole of the chance↔order axis: no randomness, no decay, a fixed interval sustained forever. The fifth is tuned to the exact ratio **3:2**, the just perfect fifth Young pursued his whole life — so the two sine waves beat against nothing. A casual listener hears a hum; a listener who knows hears a beatless just-intonation interval, a purity a digital oscillator holds effortlessly and no acoustic instrument can sustain drift-free. That double reading (P7) is the whole point, and the beatless 3:2 held to infinity is genuinely only-the-machine's to give (novelty of location, not of image).

**What it answers.** It is a deliberate counter-node to the catalogue's *4′33″ (We Do Not Listen)* — Cage's structured silence answered by its inverse, an unending tone — and to *The webpage going on and off* (after Martin Creed): where that page is *on/off forever*, this one is *on, and only on, forever*. Its lineage is La Monte Young, the Theatre of Eternal Music, and the *Dream House* of Young and Marian Zazeela (whose magenta light gives the field its colour and whose credit belongs beside his).

**Ethics (P8).** No network, no cookies, no storage, no analytics — closing the tab is a real, total ending, so no listener is ever trapped. Sound never starts without a deliberate gesture.
