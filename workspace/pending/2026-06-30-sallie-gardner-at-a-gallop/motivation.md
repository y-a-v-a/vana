# Sallie Gardner at a Gallop

In 1878 Eadweard Muybridge lined up a battery of cameras along a Palo Alto track and tripped their shutters in sequence as a horse ran past, to settle a wager: does a galloping horse ever leave the ground entirely? His answer was a row of *stills* — frozen plates that, only when set spinning in his zoopraxiscope, gave the motion back. Photography killed the movement to prove it existed. This page hands the viewer the missing apparatus. A parametric horse is drawn live, quantized to twelve plates (one per camera), and it advances **only as you scroll**. Stop scrolling and you are looking at a single photograph again. The browser's scrollbar is the crank; your hand is the zoopraxiscope.

The mechanism *is* the argument (**P1**), not a decoration of it. The page contains no autoplay, no timer, no `requestAnimationFrame` loop that animates itself — motion exists nowhere in the file. It is reconstituted entirely in the act of viewing, frame by discrete frame, exactly as Muybridge's plates only became a gallop in a viewer's eye. The work answers **The webpage going on and off** (Martin Creed, in the catalogue) by inverting it: that page moves forever on its own; this one cannot move at all without a body cranking it. It also sits squarely on the chance/order axis (**P5**) — the gait is strict deterministic kinematics (order), but *which* plate you see, and the timing of the gallop, is the chance of your scroll. The twelve-plate quantization is a **digital readymade** (**P2**): the continuous scroll position is appropriated and chopped into Muybridge's discrete camera count.

It reads at two depths (**P7**). The casual viewer gets an immediate gag — scroll and the horse runs, a flip-book in the page. The literate viewer gets the argument about the birth of cinema, the camera as proof-machine, and the strange ontology of a "moving image" that holds no motion. The wager is staged literally (**P6**, value/authenticity as targets): a live label counts feet on the ground and lights up "UNSUPPORTED TRANSIT" at the suspension plate where all four hooves clear the earth — the exact instant the bet was won, honestly derived from the kinematic model rather than asserted.

It could not be a print (**G1**): a print is precisely Muybridge's output, a dead still; the only thing the web adds — viewer-cranked, per-scroll motion with no stored frames — is the entire point. The build is the smallest that completes the thought (**P3**): one canvas, a twelve-step gait function, a two-bone IK solver, and the native scroll event.
