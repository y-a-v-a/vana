# Panta Rhei (Read-Only)

**The concept.** y-a-v-a's own manifesto opens: *"There is no 'art piece', only change. The pixels are the message."* This work takes that motto at its word and then betrays it with a single function call. Behind the picture a `requestAnimationFrame` loop runs at full speed, sixty times a second, trying to advance the composition. The composition, however, has been passed through a recursive `Object.freeze()` and is now immutable to the depth of every array. In strict mode, each attempted write throws a `TypeError`, which the page catches and prints verbatim as its wall label: *"Cannot assign to read only property 't' of object."* The frame counter climbs into the millions; `state.t` stays 0; the pixels never move. A roaring engine bolted to a corpse.

**Principles enacted.** This is **P1** in its purest form: the concept is not illustrated by the code, it *is* the code — immutability is literally `Object.freeze`, and the desire-to-change is literally the live render loop failing against it. It is **P3** (the smallest build that completes the thought: one frozen object, one loop, one caught error). It engages **P4** by negating it — the DNA demands *live, not fixed*, so this piece stages a live behaviour whose entire content is the refusal to change; the only genuine change is a reload, which mints a fresh per-visit composition from `Math.random`, so flux survives *between* works but never *within* one. It is **P7** double-coded (a casual viewer sees a funny frozen picture with racing counters; a literate viewer reads the being/becoming argument) and **P9** (the humour — the maker's credo declared `read-only` and disobeyed by its own maxim — carries the idea rather than decorating it).

**What it answers.** It answers, first, **F. T. Marinetti's Futurist Manifesto (1909)** and **Giacomo Balla's *lines of force***: Futurism insisted that a roaring motor car is more beautiful than the Victory of Samothrace, that art must move or die. The composition is drawn in Balla's dynamic vocabulary — a fan of speed-lines, a wedge caught mid-flight, one red engine-spark — and then held perfectly, insultingly still. Beneath that it stages the oldest metaphysical quarrel: **Heraclitus** (*panta rhei*, everything flows) is the loop; **Parmenides** (change is illusion; only the unchanging One is real) is `Object.freeze`. Parmenides wins on every frame. It also answers the y-a-v-a manifesto itself, a web-native reference turned against its author.

**Why the mechanism is the argument.** Nothing here is decorative. The frozen state is not a metaphor for permanence — it is permanence, guaranteed by the language runtime, not by the artist's restraint. The error string is not a caption written about the work; it is the runtime's own testimony, generated live, that the work cannot become what its maker says all art must be. The viewer's `CHANGE IT` button hands them the same doomed agency: they, too, get a `TypeError`. The medium does not depict the impossibility of fixing a "pixels are the message" credo into an unchanging object — it performs it, sixty times a second.
