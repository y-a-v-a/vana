# Salon des Refusés

A single academic canvas hangs in a gilt frame on a Salon wall. A client-side
"Jury of the Académie" convenes on load, weighs the work against the pompier
demands of 1863 — *huile sur toile*, invisible *facture*, a unique hand, a noble
subject — and refuses it on every count. As the 1863 Salon actually did, the
Jury marks the verdict with a red **R** on the *reverse*: click the painting and
it turns on its stretcher to show the stamp and the procès-verbal. The work is
shown to you only because it was rejected. Here, refusal *is* admission; were the
Jury ever to receive it, it would have to be taken down.

The mechanism is the argument (**P1**). The Jury does not read from a script — it
reads the browser. Each academic demand is judged against a live fact the runtime
actually reports: the support is `document.contentType` (`text/html`, not oil);
the *facture* is the pixel grid at the live `devicePixelRatio`; the "unique hand"
is refuted by `Ctrl/⌘+S`, the perfect costless copy; the noble subject is a page
adjudicating its own refusal. Every criterion fails for one true reason — this is
a web page, not a painting — so the rejection is honestly *derived*, not decorated
on. The new medium is refused by the old academy exactly as it was in 1863, and
that refusal is the whole work.

It answers the **Salon des Refusés, Paris 1863** — the exhibition Napoleon III
ordered for the thousands of canvases the official Jury threw out, where Manet's
*Le Déjeuner sur l'herbe* was hung and jeered. It is a **digital readymade**
(**P2**): the institutional apparatus of jurying — its rules, its red stamp, its
verso — appropriated whole and recontextualised on the `.com`. It sits on the
chance↔order axis (**P5**): a strict, deterministic verdict (order) stamped by an
ink mark that, like a real stamp, falls a little differently each time (chance),
under a faint per-visit varnish so no two viewings share an "original."

It is double-coded (**P7**): the surface gag — a painting refused by the jury,
marked with a red R, hung anyway — lands at a glance, while the literate reader
gets the inversion (rejection as the sole condition of entry), a nod to Walter
Benjamin's reproducible image, and a sly mirror held up to the very pipeline that
judges *this* file — an automated jury scoring art against hard gates (**P6, P10**:
the validator as both apparatus and subject). The build is the smallest that
completes the thought (**P3**): one canvas, one flip, four live readings, no
storage, no network, CC BY-SA. The Académie may re-examine it as often as you
like. The decision will not change.
