# Concetto Spaziale, «Attese»

A single taut monochrome fills the screen — one of Fontana's field colours, chosen by
chance per visit. Drag a deliberate stroke and the canvas *gapes*: a clean vesica opens
along your gesture and something black shows through. In 1958 Lucio Fontana stopped
painting space and started cutting through to it — the slash (*taglio*) punctured the
picture plane to assert an infinite dimension behind the canvas. Here the same gesture
asks the web's flat, honest question: what is behind a page?

The mechanism is the argument (**P1**, **P3**). The cut is not drawn *on* the surface —
it is a real hole. The coloured field is a clip-path (`clip-rule="evenodd"`); each drag
appends a lens to that path and punches it out, so a separate black layer underneath is
genuinely *revealed*, not painted. And what it reveals is the truth of the medium: a
webpage has no depth, no z-axis, nothing rendered behind `<html>` but void. Fontana
claimed infinite space behind the canvas; the browser calls the bluff — behind the page
there is only the unrendered dark. The cut both performs Spatialism and exposes it.

This is a digital readymade of a named work (**P2**), restaging Fontana's *Concetto
Spaziale, «Attese»* (1958–68) and his *White Manifesto*'s call to break the picture plane.
It rides the chance/order axis (**P5**): an ordered constraint — one clean cut, a held
breath, capped at seven so it stays *attesa* (expectation) and never becomes a scribble —
filled by the chance of your hand and the random field colour. It is live and per-visit
(**P4**): the cuts are irreversible within a visit (the web's undo withheld), but a reload
heals the canvas to a fresh colour. Only change.

It double-codes (**P7**): a casual visitor gets the satisfying, slightly transgressive act
of slashing a beautiful painting; a literate one reads Fontana, Spatialism, and a small
argument about the ontology of the screen. The delivery keeps the ethic (**P8**): open
CC BY-SA licence stated on the work, no storage, no cookies, no network request of any
kind — the void behind the page is the only thing it ships. Attribution to Lucio Fontana
and to y-a-v-a is on the wall label, where it belongs.
