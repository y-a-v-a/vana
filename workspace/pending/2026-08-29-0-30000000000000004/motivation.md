# 0.30000000000000004

Ask a computer for one tenth plus two tenths and it answers `0.30000000000000004`. Ask it to divide the whole into three equal parts and add them back, and the parts do not return the whole — they fall short (or overshoot) by a residue near 10⁻¹⁶. This work is that residue, and nothing else. The page divides the unit into any number of equal parts, sums them one at a time, and prints the machine's honest wrong answer beside the whole it was asked to reconstitute. The dividers on the bar look perfectly exact; the number underneath proves they are not. Which divisions "close" and which fail is, to the eye, unpredictable — pure order (binary arithmetic) quietly manufacturing chance.

The mechanism **is** the argument (**P1**): the difference between the whole and its reassembled parts is not illustrated by the code, it is *produced* by IEEE 754 double-precision rounding — the base-2 machine cannot store one tenth, one third, or one seventh, so every reassembly carries an irreducible error the arithmetic cannot shed. This is Duchamp's *infra-mince* (**P2**, **P5** — the digital readymade of the immeasurable, near-zero difference) computed live on the viewer's own processor. It answers a genuinely web-native found object: **0.30000000000000004.com** (Erik Wiffin), the net's deadpan monument to floating-point error, here recontextualised from "gotcha" into artwork about the limit of exactness. It also answers **Mel Bochner's *Measurement*** series: where Bochner drew the arrow lines that name a room's true dimensions, this measures a whole and finds the measurement can never fully close.

It sits on the chance/order axis at the pole of pure order (**P5**): strict arithmetic, no `Math.random`, deterministic and identical on every conforming machine — yet the order overshoots itself and leaves entropy behind. The residue rendered "as pixels" on the bar (a fraction of the whole times the bar's real width, ~10⁻¹³ px) is the render-versus-fact tension made literal: the screen rounds the gap to nothing, the number refuses to. Double-coded (**P7**): the surface gag — "computers can't add tenths" — lands instantly and is quietly funny (**P9**); underneath is a precise claim about measurement, exactness, and the crack baked into every digital whole.

The build is the smallest that completes the thought (**P3**): a slider, a divided bar, a four-line ledger, one epigraph. It could not be a print — the residue is a live computation of the medium's own number representation, regenerated per interaction and per visit (**P4**). Open licence stated on the page, no cookies, no trackers, no network; every digit is computed on the viewer's machine (**P8**). Dry cheerful-cynic voice throughout: *the machine was asked to divide the whole into equal parts and give it back — it could not.*
