# Zone de Sensibilité Picturale Immatérielle (Web Series)

In 1959–62 Yves Klein sold *Zones of Immaterial Pictorial Sensibility*: invisible regions of space, paid for in fine gold. The collector received a receipt — but the transaction was only truly complete when the buyer **burned the receipt** while Klein threw half the gold into the Seine. Owning the work meant destroying the only proof it had ever changed hands. This page is that ritual, served. The void is an International Klein Blue field; nothing is ever rendered into it. The single material object in the whole transaction is the paper receipt the page mints for you, and the only way to acquire the immaterial zone is to burn it.

The work is a **digital readymade** of a conceptual gesture (P2), and its mechanism *is* its argument (P1). The page issues a real, unique serial — the web's reflex toward order history, certificates, blockchain provenance — and then offers two buttons. **Keep the receipt**: you hold documentation and own nothing. **Burn the receipt**: the serial is wiped from memory and the page commits, deliberately, to keeping no record at all — no cookies, no `localStorage`, no server, no token to mint. Reload and the registry is blank; there was never a transaction to find. Persistence is the medium, and its refusal is the meaning. This sits firmly on the order↔chance axis (P5): the ritual is strict order, the per-visit zone number and serial are chance, and the gesture that resolves them is destruction.

It answers Klein directly, and through him answers the present: the NFT mistook Klein's receipt for the artwork and minted the proof instead of burning it. The piece never says the word — the cheerful cynic doesn't need to (P9, P7). A casual visitor gets a clean gag (buy nothing, set fire to the bill, own it now); a literate one reads the whole argument about value, authenticity, and the documentation that quietly replaces the thing it documents (P6). Minimal build, client-side only, the smallest gesture that completes the thought (P3): International Klein Blue, one receipt, one match.
