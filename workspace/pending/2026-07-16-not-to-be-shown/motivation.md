# Not to Be Shown

Hilma af Klint painted abstraction before anyone had a word for it, then stipulated that her work not be exhibited until twenty years after her death. She died in 1944; the art world took closer to seventy years to look. This page holds a painting and enforces her instruction literally: a generative composition after *The Ten Largest* is rendered in canvas only when the device's own clock has passed the embargo date — the centenary of her death, 5 October 2044. Until then the viewer gets a wax seal, a live countdown, and the plain admission that the whole lock is one settable integer.

The **mechanism is the argument** (P1). The work is not *about* an embargo; the embargo *is* a `new Date()` comparison run every second. Nothing is hidden by decoration — the composition genuinely does not draw until the release moment arrives, and it will draw the instant the clock says so. That is why the piece could not be a print (G1): a print cannot withhold itself, count down, or notice that you have advanced your calendar. It is the **digital readymade** (P2) with the operating-system clock as the found gatekeeper, and it stays **live** (P4) — checking on load, on tab-focus, and on a one-second interval, painting fresh per visit and keeping nothing.

The single integer is the joke and the point (P7, P9). To the casual visitor it reads as *"aw, it's locked — wait, I can just cheat?"*; to the literate one it is af Klint's real embargo, plus a Duchampian **infrathin**: the difference between a masterpiece sealed for a century and one shown now is smaller than a hair — it is a number in a settings panel. Institutional gatekeeping of "hidden" art, the DNA's recurring theme of value and authenticity (P6), turns out to be theatre anyone can walk through. The cheerful cynic notes that recognition, too, is just a date.

The composition itself sits on the **chance/order axis** (P5): af Klint claimed her forms were dictated to her — an automatism, pure chance — yet they arrive as strict concentric ovoids, dualities and radial spokes, pure order. The generator stages exactly that tension, re-throwing the botanical spokes and private letterforms on every visit while holding the ruled geometry fixed. It answers On Kawara's date works and this catalogue's other clock pieces (*Today*, *Time Clock Piece*) by inverting them: those mark the present as it passes; this one refuses the present until a future it will almost certainly outlive.
