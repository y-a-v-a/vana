# Pure Colour

In 1921 Aleksandr Rodchenko hung three monochromes — *Pure Red Colour, Pure Yellow Colour, Pure Blue Colour* — and declared painting finished: "I reduced painting to its logical conclusion." A century later the medium that finishes painting is already installed on every device: the operating system's native `<input type="color">` dialog. The whole inheritance of pigment, gesture and taste has been compressed into a stock widget that produces exactly one flat colour. This work hands that widget the title of *painting*.

The mechanism is the argument (**P1**). The visible square is not a picture of a colour — it *is* the `<input type="color">`. Clicking it does not let you paint; it summons the operating system, and the OS repaints the work. The viewer makes no marks, mixes nothing, and skills nothing: the deskilled, automated end of painting that Rodchenko announced is performed literally by handing the brush to a system dialog (**P2**, the digital readymade — the OS chrome itself is the found object). The chosen colour floods the entire viewport and even the browser chrome via `theme-color`, so the page does not *show* a monochrome, it *becomes* one. The moment you pick, the wall label flips from "Choose the last colour" to "It's over."

This is the smallest build that completes the thought (**P3**): one native input, one flood-fill, one verdict. It sits at the *order* pole of the chance/order axis (**P5**) — a single deterministic value, the logical conclusion — and it points squarely at value and authenticity (**P6**): the "last painting" is now an unauthored default-grey form control anyone can overwrite in a click. It double-codes (**P7**): a casual visitor finds a colour toy; a literate one finds Rodchenko's death sentence executed by a UI component.

It answers Rodchenko's *Pure Red / Pure Yellow / Pure Blue Colour* (1921) and the monochrome lineage behind it, but it could not be a print: it depends on the browser invoking the host operating system's own colour dialog, an event with no paper equivalent (**G1**). Open licence, attribution, no cookies, no trackers, no network (**P8**).
