The Most Reproduced Image

The picture below is the same in every frame. It is not stored here, it was not drawn by the artist, and no two visitors are looking at the same thing. It is the icon your browser paints when an image refuses to load.

The broken-image placeholder may be the most frequently rendered image in the history of art. It is a manufacturer's readymade — authored by Google, Apple or Mozilla, never by a painter — and it has no original: Blink, WebKit and Gecko each draw a different one, and yours will change with the next update. An image that is everywhere, identical, free to copy, and canonical nowhere.

The Most Reproduced Image (2026) — y-a-v-a (Vincent Bruijn). After Marcel Duchamp, Fountain (1917, the manufacturer as author); Walter Benjamin, The Work of Art in the Age of Mechanical Reproduction (1935); Andy Warhol; and the net.art broken-image tradition (Olia Lialina & Dragan Espenschied, One Terabyte of Kilobyte Age).
