# Vir Heroicus Sublimis (Overflow)

Barnett Newman spent years on the *zip* — the single vertical band that splits and
fuses his color fields, the line he called the moment of creation. *Vir Heroicus
Sublimis* (1950–51) is eight metres wide; Newman taped a note to the wall asking
viewers to stand *close*, so the red overflows their vision and the zip becomes a
place to stand rather than a thing to look at. This work observes that every web
browser already ships a zip: the vertical scrollbar. It is a saturated line on a
field, and it appears for exactly Newman's reason — because the field is bigger
than the frame that holds it. So the work is a sublime red page taller than your
screen, and the only mark on it is the browser's own scrollbar, styled to be the
zip.

This is a **digital readymade** (P2): an existing, overlooked piece of cultural
machinery — browser chrome — recontextualised so its literal function becomes the
artwork. And it is **concept-is-execution** (P1): the zip is not *drawn* to
resemble a scrollbar, it *is* the scrollbar. The mechanism is the argument — the
zip exists if and only if the document overflows the viewport (CSS `overflow`),
which is precisely Newman's condition of the sublime: a field too large to be
taken in at once. To read the zip you must use it; using it carries you into the
red. The drag-handle that conceptual painting reached for by force of will is, on
the web, the default affordance for coping with excess. The sublime now has a grip.

It answers Newman directly, with a side-glance at **Duchamp's readymade** and the
**net.art** habit of treating browser/OS chrome as material (it joins this DNA's
line of chrome works — favicon, fullscreen, print sheet, color picker — by claiming
the one widget those left untouched). It rides the chance/order axis on the order
side: nothing here is random; the only variable is your viewport and how far you
scroll, reported live as a percentage *into the field* (P7 double-coding — a bold
red page with a fat colored bar for the casual eye; Newman's zip and his standing
instruction for the literate one). Where a system hides its scrollbar (Mac overlay,
touch), a small script repaints the one the machine owes you — the zip withheld,
returned — so the readymade survives platforms that bury it.

Built as the smallest thing that completes the thought (P3): a tall page, a styled
scrollbar, one line of "depth" readout, an overlay fallback. No images, no fonts,
no network, no storage (P8) — stated CC BY-SA, attributed to y-a-v-a after Newman.
The tone is the cheerful cynic (P9): the most transcendent line in postwar
painting turns out to have been a scroll affordance the whole time, and it still
works.
