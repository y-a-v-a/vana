# I Went (:visited)

A gallery checklist of canonical artworks. You are asked to tick the ones you have seen with your own eyes. Each line is a same-page hash link pointing at its own `id`, so clicking it enters that address into your browsing history — and the browser paints it violet, the oldest web colour for a place you have already been. Beside every line the page runs an honest detector: `getComputedStyle(link).color`, checked against the violet of `:visited`. The number it reports is always zero. It will keep reading; it will keep saying zero.

The point is the asymmetry, and the asymmetry is not decoration — it is a law of the medium. Since 2010 (L. D. Baron / Mozilla) every browser deliberately reports `:visited` links to script *as if they were unvisited*, to shut down CSS history-sniffing. So the record of your looking is fully visible to you (the violet in the left column) and structurally unreadable to the page (the uniform ink swatches in the right column). This is **P1** in its purest form: the mechanism *is* the argument. The browser's privacy mitigation, not any drawing on top of it, performs the idea that your history of attention is yours and withheld from the institution. It is also **P8** made literal — the anti-surveillance ethic is not a footer promise here, it is the enforced substrate; the page could not spy even if it wanted to. **P2**, the digital readymade: the visited-link colour, that most ambient default of the web, is appropriated whole. **P4** (live, per-interaction), **P7** (a checklist gag for the casual eye; the history-leak fix for the literate one), and **P9** (the dry punchline of a counter that reads honestly and is honestly kept ignorant).

It answers On Kawara's **I Went** (1968–1979), the diaristic maps of every route he walked, and its siblings **I Read** and **I Met** — a private, obsessive, unmonetised record of a life's movements. Kawara's ledger was his alone to keep. Here the browser enforces exactly that solitude: you may keep the diary of what you have seen, but the page opposite you — the gallery, the platform, the analytics — is forbidden by the engine from copying it down. The colour violet does the remembering; the privacy sandbox does the forgetting.

The mechanism could not survive as a print. On paper both columns would simply be ink, and the withholding — the whole subject — would be invisible. It exists only because a live browser both applies `:visited` to your eye and refuses it to the script in the same instant.
