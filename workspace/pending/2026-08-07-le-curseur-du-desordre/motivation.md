# Le Curseur du Désordre

The whole practice orbits a single axis: **order** (strict rule-based systems) versus **chance** (entropy admitted into the system). The DNA names it explicitly (P5) and crowns Kenneth Martin and Malevich as its saints — but the one artist who spent a career dialling that exact axis by hand is missing from the catalogue: **Vera Molnár**, the mother of generative computer art, who from the 1960s asked how much disorder a grid can take before it stops being a composition and becomes noise. Her answer, famously, was small: *1% de Désordre*. This work hands her dial to you.

A field of nested squares is generated twice over. Its geometry is pure **order** — four corners, perfectly inset, deterministic. Its disorder is a per-visit random field, one fixed offset per vertex, seeded once and held only in RAM. The native `<input type="range">` between the words **ORDRE** and **HASARD** scales that random field from ×0 to ×1. At zero the offsets vanish and the grid is rigid Mondrian; drag right and the *same* chance field grows until the squares dissolve into scribble. The slider is not a control *over* the artwork — the slider **is** the chance/order axis, made into a thing you drag with your own hand (P1, P5). The live verdict reports where you are standing on it, and quietly insists that "art" occupies only a narrow band near 1%: both ends — perfect order and pure chance — are disqualified as "not yet a Molnár."

This answers Molnár's *(Dés)Ordres* and *Interruptions* directly, and talks back to y-a-v-a's own **A Chance of Order** (2021, after Kenneth Martin) and to **Arranged According to the Laws of Chance** (Arp): where those fix a single throw, this one refuses to. Its novelty is ontological (P3) — the artwork is not a *point* on the axis but the *axis itself, traversable*. A print can only ever be one disorder value; only a live, interactive, per-visit render can be the continuum (G1).

The mechanism *is* the argument, not decoration: the number that separates a masterpiece from wallpaper is exposed as a slider value you can read off in percent, and the machine cheerfully tells you when you have dragged the art out of the picture (P7, P9). Order and chance are the two engines; here they share one track, and you are the cursor.

**Principles:** P1 (concept = execution: the axis is the control), P3 (smallest build; the ontology is the point), P4 (live, per-visit chance held only in RAM), P5 (the chance↔order axis, staged literally), P7 (surface: a slider makes squares messy; depth: art as a thin band of controlled disorder), P9 (the dry verdict carries the idea).

**References:** Vera Molnár, *1% de Désordre* / *(Dés)Ordres* (1976) and *Interruptions* (1968–69); Kenneth Martin, *Chance and Order*; Piet Mondrian; and y-a-v-a's own *A Chance of Order* (2021).
