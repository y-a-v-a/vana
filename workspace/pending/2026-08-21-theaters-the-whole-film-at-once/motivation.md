# Theaters (The Whole Film at Once)

Hiroshi Sugimoto's *Theaters* (1978– ) are made with one gesture: open the camera shutter as the feature begins, close it as the credits end. The film — ninety minutes of drama, faces, weather, murder — deposits all its light onto a single frame, and what develops is a screen of pure, even white. The narrative is fully present and completely gone. This piece serves that gesture back to the web as a live behavior instead of a finished photograph.

A generated film plays on the screen — a different luminous scene each visit — and the screen keeps *everything*. The exposure canvas holds the running mean of every frame it has ever shown: each new frame is composited at alpha `1/n`, which is exactly the incremental average. As `n` grows the picture stops changing and settles into a glowing, near-uniform rectangle. A live readout reports the screen's luminance and its standard deviation σ; watch long enough and σ collapses toward zero — the image measurably erasing itself. The corner inset labelled *the film* shows the raw frames still churning, so the casual viewer gets the gag (a movie dissolving into a blank screen) and the literate one gets Sugimoto's paradox: **the more you are shown, the less there is to see.**

The mechanism *is* the argument (**P1**). Nothing decorative averages the frames — the averaging is literally the artwork; the blankness is not drawn, it is *arrived at* by summing light, the same physics as Sugimoto's open shutter. It sits on the chance↔order axis (**P5**): chaotic, colliding, cutting scenes (chance) integrate into a single ordered field (order), and because it asymptotes but never perfectly resolves, the last sliver of difference is a small infrathin (Duchamp) that never quite closes. It is a live, regenerating system, one edition per visit, erased on rewind and never repeated (**P4**), and it could not be a print: a print would be Sugimoto's endpoint, whereas the work here is the *dissolving in real time* — the thing paper cannot hold (**G1**). The voice is the dry cheerful cynic (**P9**): "sit through to the end and the end is a blank."

**Reference answered:** Hiroshi Sugimoto, *Theaters* (1978– ). Adjacent lineage: On Kawara's durational time works and the vanitas tradition of duration-as-erasure, and Duchamp's infrathin for the residue that never averages away.

**Ethics (P8):** self-contained single file, no network, no cookies, no trackers, no storage; all light is computed and discarded on your own machine. CC BY 4.0, attributed to y-a-v-a after Hiroshi Sugimoto.
