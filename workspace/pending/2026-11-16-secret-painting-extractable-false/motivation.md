In 1967–68 Mel Ramsden (Art & Language) made *Secret Painting*: a black square beside a text panel declaring that its content is invisible, its character and dimension permanently secret, "known only to the artist." It was a bluff you had to take on trust. This page cashes the bluff. On every visit it mints a real painting (a grid of crypto-random colours, of random size), encrypts it with AES-GCM under a key generated with `extractable: false`, zeroes the plaintext, and hangs the 784-byte ciphertext as a 28 × 28 square in which every byte is shown at 0–7 out of 255. So the black square is the encrypted content itself, not a stand-in for it.

The mechanism makes the argument (P1). Web Crypto's non-extractable key exists and works inside the tab, yet the page that made it cannot read it back. "Ask the artist" calls `exportKey` live, and the browser answers with an `InvalidAccessError`. The painting is padded to a fixed length, so even the ciphertext's size gives away nothing about the "dimension." Art & Language had to assert the secret. The browser enforces it, and the browser is the only artist that knows. Reload, and that secret is lost to everyone, including its keeper (P4: only change). The source is open and CC-licensed and the content can never be recovered. That tension is the joke, and it is also a real point about open media (P8).

The piece is a digital readymade of a conceptual-art classic (P2) built from one browser primitive (P3). It reads at two depths (P7). The casual viewer gets a black square that will not tell you what it is. The literate viewer gets Art & Language against Malevich's *Black Square*, with authorship handed over to a security API. The cryptographic randomness under a strict, fixed-format rule puts it on the chance/order axis (P5). It answers y-a-v-a's *Maleglitch* (2010), where data turned into a Suprematist image, by doing the reverse: an image turns back into data and is hidden in black. It also answers *Zone de Sensibilité Picturale Immatérielle* (2026). There a secret was held and then burned. Here it is kept, and kept unreadable.
