# Magnet TV (Bring Your Own Field)

In 1965 Nam June Paik stood a magnet on top of a working television and video
art began. The magnet distorted the deflection of the cathode ray, folding the
broadcast raster into a living abstraction that lasted exactly as long as the
magnet stayed put. Remove the magnet and the newsreader returned. The picture
was never a picture; it was a *field*, held.

This is that gesture ported to the only screen most people now own. The set
broadcasts nothing but its own test pattern — SMPTE bars, a black-to-white
sweep, "NO SIGNAL" — the image a channel shows precisely when it has nothing to
show, which is the web's condition too: **we do not search, we serve.** Then
your pointer becomes the magnet. A per-frame client-side warp bends the raster
around wherever you press — magnifying, swirling, breathing with a faint pulse
so the field feels live — and the instant you lift your hand, broadcast order
snaps back, unbruised. Nothing is stored, so no distortion you make will ever
exist a second time (P4).

The mechanism *is* the argument (P1, P3). The distortion is not a decorative
filter sitting on top of a picture; there is no picture to sit on, only ordered
signal and the field of your attention deforming it. The warp exists only while
your hand is present and vanishes with it — Paik's magnet, exactly, translated
into pointer capture and a canvas the browser refuses to remember. It sits
cleanly on the chance/order axis (P5): the test pattern is pure order, the
broadcast default; the pointer drags chance-shaped folds through it that the
system will not let you keep. The double coding (P7) is a colourful tube you can
poke on the surface, and video art's origin story underneath — the claim that
the screen is not a window but a manipulable material.

**References answered:** Nam June Paik, *Magnet TV* (1965), and behind it his
line that the cathode ray tube would replace the canvas. It also nods to the
broadcast test card as a found readymade (P2) — the honest image of an empty
channel.

as readymade), P3 (smallest build — one canvas, one warp, no libraries), P4
