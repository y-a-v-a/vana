# What You See Is What You See

In 1966 Frank Stella gave conceptual art one of its most quotable refusals: *"What you see is what you see."* His *Black Paintings* (1958–60) were the proof — bands of black enamel separated by thin lines of bare canvas, the stripes not depicting anything but simply echoing the shape of the stretcher. The structure was **deductive**: every internal line was reasoned outward from the framing edge, so the picture contained no illusion, no reference, nothing behind the surface.

This work hands the stretcher to your browser. A canvas redraws concentric bands that are computed *solely* from `window.innerWidth` and `window.innerHeight` — band width is a fixed fraction of the shorter side, each rectangle is inset from the live viewport edge, the gaps show the unprimed ground beneath. There is no stored image, no asset, no external request; the painting is literally the deduction of its own support. Resize the window and it re-reasons itself from scratch, a different number of bands, the same logic. This is the **concept as execution** (P1): the picture is nothing but its frame thinking out loud, which is exactly what "what you see is what you see" demands.

It sits firmly on the **order** end of the chance/order axis (P5): wholly rule-based, deterministic, identical in any browser at the same size — the antithesis of the catalogue's glitch and entropy pieces, and the deadpan opposite of *The Responsive Eye* (Bridget Riley), where the device's pixel grid is conscripted to produce an illusion. Here the device is conscripted to *abolish* illusion. It answers Stella directly (P2, digital readymade of a position), and quietly answers net.art's old question of where a picture lives: not in a file, but in the geometry of the window holding it.

The dry joke (P9) is that a painting famous for having no depth, no secret, no "besides," is here a few lines of arithmetic with genuinely nothing stored — the most honest possible monochrome, because there is no image to be honest about. It reads at the surface as a clean hard-edge picture and at depth as Stella's whole anti-metaphysical argument, double-coded (P7). Smallest build that completes the thought (P3): one canvas, one resize handler, no dependencies. Open licensed, no cookies, no tracking (P8).
