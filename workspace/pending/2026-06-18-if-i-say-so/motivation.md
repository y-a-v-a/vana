# If I Say So

In 1961 Iris Clert asked the artists in her gallery for a portrait. Robert Rauschenberg sent a telegram: **"THIS IS A PORTRAIT OF IRIS CLERT IF I SAY SO."** No likeness, no paint — the portrait was constituted entirely by the speech act. Authorship, sitter, and authenticity were settled by fiat. The work is a declaration that declares itself.

This piece relocates that declaration to the one place on the web with absolute authority over a page: the URL. The sitter of the portrait is bound to `location.hash`. Whatever stands after the `#` is who the portrait is *of* — read live on load and on every `hashchange`. The small input field does nothing but write to the address bar; the address bar alone paints the portrait. **Saying so becomes typing the address.** That binding is the argument, not its illustration (P1): Rauschenberg's piece is about authorship-by-assertion, and on the web the most authoritative assertion you can make about a page is its URL. Change the URL, change the sitter — and since anyone can change a URL, the work quietly demonstrates that this authority is free, transferable, and backed by nothing but the saying (P6, value/authenticity).

It is a digital readymade of a conceptual readymade (P2): a 1961 telegram re-staged through native browser plumbing — `location.hash`, `hashchange`, `history.replaceState`. It could not survive as a print, because the print could only ever name one sitter; here the sitter is a variable the viewer assigns, so the work is a behavior, not an artifact (P4), regenerating per address. It reads at two depths (P7): the surface gag is a gallery portrait of literally anyone, "likeness not included"; underneath is the nominalist claim that a name plus an assertion is sufficient to make art — and the dry joke that the notary is the address bar. Minimal build, smallest gesture that completes the thought (P3): one sentence, one frame, one `#`.

The voice is the cheerful cynic. The wall label lists the medium as "the address bar" and the authenticity as "my word." It answers Rauschenberg's telegram with the web's version of saying-so, and sits on the *order* end of the chance/order axis (P5): no randomness, just a rule — the URL is the sitter — applied without exception, including to you.
