Damien Hirst's spot paintings are famous for not being painted by Damien Hirst: he set the rule (a grid, equal spots, no colour repeated) and assistants did the work, a fact he once put as "the best spot painting you can have by me is one painted by Rachel." *Off the Main Thread* takes the browser's own division of labour as its readymade. The main thread, where a page's "self" lives, lays out the grid, writes the label and signs. Every spot is painted by a Web Worker, a separate thread hired once per CPU core (`navigator.hardwareConcurrency`). Your machine's core count sets the size of the studio.

The handover is literal, not a metaphor (P1). Each spot's canvas goes to an assistant through `transferControlToOffscreen()`, and after that the browser forbids the main thread from touching it. The "Let the artist paint one" button tries anyway and prints the real `InvalidStateError` the engine throws. "Spots by the artist: 0" is a fact the platform enforces. It is not a joke the page tells. Authorship, value and the signature (P6) come apart on a red sold dot.

The work sits on the chance/order axis (P5). The order side is the artist's: grid, palette and rule. The chance side is the workers' own: they finish in a race order nobody wrote, and because workers share no memory they cannot see each other's spots. So Hirst's "no colour repeats" rule is broken by structure, and a live counter tallies the repeats. The rule-maker's idea and the hands that carry it out run in isolated threads, and the system gives them no way to agree. Each visit hires a new studio and paints a new picture; reload lets the staff go (P4). It is a digital readymade of the studio system (P2), small enough to fit on one wall label (P3).

On the surface it is a cheerful spot painting with a dry label (P7). The literate reading follows the line from Rubens's workshop to Koons's fabricators to Hirst, and answers y-a-v-a's *For the Love of Fame* (2009) and *Three Ball Total Equilibrium Slot Machine* (2020): the market pays for the name, and the name has been forbidden from holding the brush. No cookies, no storage, no network; the worker code is built inline from a Blob, and the work is CC BY-SA (P8).
