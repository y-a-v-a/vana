# COPY / COPY

Two words sit one above the other, set in the same serif at the same size: **COPY** and **COPY**. They are, glyph for glyph, the same picture. One is the English word, spelled with the Latin letters C‑O‑P‑Y. The other is a *picture* of that word, assembled from the Cyrillic letters Es‑O‑Er‑U (С‑О‑Р‑У), which happen to share the same outlines. The browser's text engine draws both from the same font, so on screen they are indistinguishable — and, crucially, on paper they would be *equally* indistinguishable, which is exactly why this work cannot be a print. The difference is real, permanent, and located only where the eye cannot go: the byte stream. A forensic ledger reports `word_1 === word_2` → **false**, and that even Unicode normalization (NFC and the aggressive NFKC fold) refuses to merge them. The confusion is not a bug the standard will fix; it is a fact of the medium.

This is Duchamp's **inframince** (infrathin) rendered literally (**P1** — the mechanism *is* the concept, not a decoration of it). His stock example was "the difference between two objects cast from the same mould": a difference that is certain to exist yet impossible to perceive. Here the browser's glyph rasteriser is the mould — it stamps two distinct codepoint sequences into one identical image — and the inframince is the delta the machine measures but no viewer can see. The "Reveal the difference" button does the only thing that can expose it: it steps outside vision into script metadata and tints the impostor gold. The web-native readymade being appropriated (**P2**) is the Unicode *confusables* table and the IDN homograph attack — the same trick phishers use to make `аpple.com` look like `apple.com`.

It answers a running thread in the catalogue about the original and its copy — *The Original* (2026), *The Difference (after Sturtevant)* (2026), *The Most Reproduced Image* (2026) — and turns on **P6** (value / authenticity): Walter Benjamin located the aura of the artwork in its unreproducible here‑and‑now, and Sherrie Levine's *After Walker Evans* proved a copy can be pixel‑perfect and still be a different object with a different author. COPY / COPY collapses that whole argument into four letters: the perfect copy is achievable, indistinguishable, and *still not the same string*. The aura survives — as an invisible integer.

Double‑coded per **P7**: the casual viewer gets a clean typographic gag (two identical words, "spot the difference," you can't). The literate viewer gets inframince, mechanical reproduction, and the phishing exploit sitting in one object. The joke (**P9**) is the cheerful‑cynic delivery of a genuine point about identity in a medium where the copy is free and flawless — and the smallest build (**P3**) that completes the thought: two words, one comparison, no libraries, no requests, nothing stored.
