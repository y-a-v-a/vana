# Composition in Red, Yellow and Blue (32 × 32)

The whole artwork lives in the browser tab. The page itself is an empty room with a wall label that points upward: the painting is the little square next to the title, 32 by 32 pixels, and that is its full size. There is no larger version to click through to, no "view original." The most ignored rectangle in the entire interface — the favicon, the spot reserved for corporate logos — is here promoted to the only gallery on the page.

The mechanism *is* the argument (P1). Mondrian spent thirty years reducing painting to a black grid and three primaries on white, chasing a universal order. The browser hands every page a mandatory raster grid of its own — the favicon — and it is already Neoplastic: orthogonal, primary-ready, ruthlessly small. So the work draws a real recursive Mondrian directly into that grid via `<canvas>` → `toDataURL` → `<link rel="icon">`. The favicon's pixel lattice and Mondrian's compositional lattice are made the same object; the reduction Mondrian *aspired* to, the browser simply *imposes*. That collapse cannot survive as a print — a print has no tab, no chrome, no 32×32 mandate (G1).

This sits squarely on the chance/order axis (P5). The subdivision is strict rule (order); where each cut falls, when the recursion stops, and which few cells take a primary is entropy (chance) — Mondrian's grammar run by a coin. It is also a piece about *location* rather than image, which is the kind of novelty the DNA prizes: a new place for the artwork to live (P4). Each visit paints a fresh composition; closing the tab erases it forever, with nothing stored and nothing sent (P8).

It answers **Piet Mondrian** (*Composition with Red, Blue and Yellow*, c. 1930 — public domain), borrowing his exact title format and appending the punchline dimension. It also nods to net.art's habit of treating browser chrome as material. Double-coded (P7): the casual viewer gets a dry visual gag — "the painting is in my tab and it's tiny" — while the literate viewer reads the reductio: museum-grade modernism delivered at the resolution of a brand mark, in the margin where attention never goes, free and gone on close.

Principles: **P1** (concept = execution), **P2** (digital readymade — the favicon as found frame), **P3** (smallest build that completes the thought), **P4** (live, per-visit, erased on exit), **P5** (chance within order), **P7** (double-coding).
