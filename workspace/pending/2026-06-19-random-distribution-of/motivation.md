# Random Distribution of π

In 1960 François Morellet made *Répartition aléatoire de 40 000 carrés suivant les chiffres pairs et impairs d'un annuaire de téléphone*: a 200×200 grid in which every square was decided not by him but by the digits of a phone book — even, one colour; odd, another. He abolished the artist's hand by outsourcing every choice to a found number. This work answers Morellet's directly, swapping his finite, regional, paper directory for the one phone book the web can compute rather than look up: the decimal expansion of π. Each digit, taken in order, fills a square (odd → ink) or clears it (even → paper). No mark is chosen; the chooser has no end and belongs to no one.

The mechanism is the argument (**P1**). An unbounded BigInt spigot streams the exact digits of π forever, client-side; a scroll handler asks it for more squares whenever you reach the bottom, so the picture is never finished — Morellet's 40,000 squares become infinite, because π is. This is the thing only the web gives Morellet: not a wall of a fixed size but a painting that keeps writing itself as long as you keep looking (**P4**). Crucially the stream is *deterministic*: this exact field renders identically in every browser, on every screen, for every visitor, forever. That is the bite (**P7**). It looks like static — the canonical look of chance — and it is in fact the most fixed image there is: reproducible "randomness," the chance/order axis collapsed into a single object where the two are indistinguishable (**P5**). Chance you can reproduce is just order you didn't expect.

π is the digital readymade here (**P2**): an external given, never invented, served not searched. The build is the smallest that completes the thought (**P3**) — a spigot, a parity test, a grid, a scroll listener — and the voice stays dry rather than mystical about it (**P9**): the wall text quietly insists this noise is the opposite of noise. It answers Morellet, and stands beside *A Chance of Order* (Kenneth Martin) on the same axis from the other end — where Martin rolled dice for genuine contingency, this piece uses a number whose digits only *look* contingent and are in truth completely determined and shared by everyone alive.
