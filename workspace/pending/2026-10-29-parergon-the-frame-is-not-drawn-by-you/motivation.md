# Parergon (The Frame Is Not Drawn by You)

A small per-visit painting sits on the page with nothing around it. Click it with a pointer and it takes focus — the page attests `:focus = true` — yet no frame appears. Reach the *same* pixels with the Tab key and the browser draws its native focus outline around them, and the caption flips: *now it is a work*. The painting never changed a pixel. Only its border did, and you did not draw it.

The concept is Derrida's **parergon** (*The Truth in Painting*, 1978), itself read out of Kant's third Critique (1790): the frame is neither inside the work (the *ergon*) nor outside it — it is the supplement that decides where the work ends and the world begins. On the web that supplement already exists as a readymade: the user-agent **focus outline**. And the browser governs its appearance through the `:focus-visible` heuristic, which grants the ring to keyboard arrivals and withholds it from pointer clicks — a judgment made *inside the engine*, that this page cannot reliably forge, override, or even author. We deliberately leave the outline unstyled so each OS supplies its own frame (**P2**, digital readymade; the vendor-drawn tradition).

The **mechanism is the argument** (**P1**): I do not illustrate "framing makes it art" with a drawn border — I let the literal operation of `:focus-visible` perform it. The identical ergon is a picture or a work depending on a modality heuristic buried in the browser, and the live `matches(':focus-visible')` readout is the page honestly reporting a verdict it is not allowed to make. This answers Duchamp's framing/readymade logic and sits beside works that treat institutional threshold as material (Brian O'Doherty's white cube, Manzoni's pedestal) — but relocates the threshold from the gallery to the browser's own accessibility heuristic. It is double-coded (**P7**): the casual visitor gets the dry gag (*mouse users get no art*), the literate one gets the parergon argument. Minimal build, no persistence, no network (**P3**, **P8**).

Chance enters only as texture (**P5**): the ergon is minted per visit with `crypto.getRandomValues` and held in RAM, so the thing being framed-or-not is itself irreproducible — reload and the browser is adjudicating a different painting. References answered: Jacques Derrida, *The Truth in Painting / Le parergon* (1978); Immanuel Kant, *Critique of the Power of Judgment* (1790); Marcel Duchamp / the readymade.
