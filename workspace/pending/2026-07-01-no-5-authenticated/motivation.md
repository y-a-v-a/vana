# No. 5 (Authenticated)

The browser drips a Pollock from a random seed, then measures the fractal dimension of the paint it just laid down and issues itself a certificate of authenticity. Every splatter passes. The catalogue number is the seed; the price of comparison is the $140,000,000 that *No. 5, 1948* fetched in 2006. It is a machine authenticating its own noise as a masterpiece.

The reference it answers is not only Jackson Pollock but the pseudo-science built around him: Richard Taylor, Micolich and Jonas proposed in *Nature* (1999) that a genuine Pollock could be told from a fake by its fractal dimension, and Katherine Jones-Smith and Harsh Mathur replied (*Nature*, 2006) that a child's doodle scores just as well. The work stages that quarrel live. It also stands in Duchamp's readymade lineage (P2) and squarely on the value/authenticity target (P6) — the recurring "but is it art?" pointed at the market's hunger to *verify* authorship with a number.

The mechanism is the argument (P1, P3). The exact box-counting algorithm the connoisseurs used runs client-side on the very ImageData the page generated — `getImageData` reads back the drips, counts occupied boxes across scales, and a least-squares slope becomes D. Because any busy drip lands in Taylor's 1.10–1.90 window, the verdict is honestly computed and reliably "AUTHENTIC." The certifier and the certified are one object; the test that was meant to catch forgers cannot tell a Pollock from a random number generator. This could not be a print: the painting is per-visit chance (P5), the authentication is a live computation on those same pixels, and "Paint another" mints an infinite edition of certified originals.

Double-coded (P7): the casual viewer gets a pretty drip stamped with a green seal and a joke; the literate viewer gets the collapse of algorithmic connoisseurship and the circularity of self-issued provenance. The tone is the cheerful cynic (P9) — the fine print quietly confesses the method was debunked seven years after it was proposed, and shrugs.
