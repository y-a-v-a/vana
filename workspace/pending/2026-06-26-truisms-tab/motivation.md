# Truisms (Tab)

Jenny Holzer's *Truisms* (1977–79) began as anonymous flyers and ended on the great LED tickers of public space — Times Square's Spectacolor board — where her flat, contradictory one-liners ("ABUSE OF POWER COMES AS NO SURPRISE," "MONEY CREATES TASTE") borrowed the authority of a stock ticker or a public-service announcement. The medium was the argument: received wisdom reads as fact when it scrolls in an official-looking strip you didn't choose and can't switch off. This work relocates the truisms to the web's only equivalent strip — `document.title`, the browser tab — and lets the chrome itself speak them.

The mechanism *is* the message (**P1**). The tab title is the smallest, most public, most ever-present text slot the browser owns: you don't control it, it carries the authority of "the system," and — crucially — it stays visible when you switch tabs or minimise. So Holzer's signage follows you out of the page and across your whole browser, preaching to your peripheral vision exactly as her LED boards preached to commuters who never asked. The 1990s "scrolling-title" gag — a piece of net.art junk — is repurposed as her ticker. The visible page is deliberately demoted to a footnote that states one line at a time and points, with a small arrow, back up at the tab where the real work is.

It is a digital readymade (**P2**): Holzer's exact texts appropriated and recontextualised through a web behaviour, in the Duchampian line the catalogue keeps answering. It is live, not fixed (**P4**) — a perpetual character-clock marquee, reshuffled per visit so the order of "truth" is itself a throw of chance against the marquee's strict order (**P5**). It double-codes (**P7**): the casual viewer gets a funny tab editorialising at them; the literate viewer gets Holzer's critique of the anonymous voice of authority, now issuing from browser chrome. The humour is the carrier wave, not the point (**P9**).

Why it could not be a print: a print has no tab. The work depends on a behaviour the browser performs *for* you and *outside* the page — a string the chrome displays in your peripheral attention, persisting through your inattention. Nothing is rendered that a screenshot would preserve; the venue is the title bar, and the title bar only exists live.

It answers Holzer's *Truisms* and her Spectacolor signage, and it joins the catalogue's running interest in browser chrome as material (the favicon-Mondrian, the title-flip of *Acid Action Painting*, the theme-color of *Pure Colour*) — but here the chrome is not a frame for the work; the chrome is the gallery, the LED board, and the voice all at once.
