# The Artist Is Present

In 2010 Marina Abramović sat motionless at a table in MoMA for three months while
strangers sat opposite, one at a time, and simply held her gaze. The artwork was
not an object; it was the shared, undocumented duration of two people being present
to each other. This page rebuilds that encounter with the only material the web can
honestly offer: your attention, measured against your own restlessness.

The artist here looks straight out of the screen at you, and a clock counts the
seconds you sustain the meeting. But the web is a machine built to break presence —
it wants you to scroll, twitch the cursor, glance at another tab, resize, reply to a
notification. So every one of those gestures is treated as what it is: an exit. A
`pointermove`, `wheel`, `keydown`, or `touchmove` resets the clock to zero (you
flinched); a `blur` or `visibilitychange` empties the seat entirely (you left the
room). To receive the work you must do the single thing this medium trains you never
to do: stop moving, stop looking away, and stay. Sustained stillness is answered by
a slow warmth and the artist's quiet blinking; the slightest motion snuffs it.

This is P1 — concept *is* execution. The mechanism does not illustrate presence, it
enforces it: the same event stream that ad-tech mines for "engagement" is inverted
into a test of un-engagement, of stillness. It is P4 (a live behaviour, never a fixed
artifact) and P8/P3 in the same gesture — the piece keeps no cookie, no storage, no
log, sends nothing, and even its "longest gaze" lives only in memory and dies on
reload, because a presence that is recorded is no longer presence. It answers
Abramović directly (P2, the digital readymade of a canonical performance) and sits on
the chance/order axis (P5) at the human end: the durational, unrepeatable now.

The narrative node: where Vito Acconci's *Following Piece* had the cursor *pursue*
(surveillance as motion), this asks the opposite — that you and the work both hold
still and simply co-exist. The cheerful cynicism is the setup: an interface that does
nothing, produces nothing, sells nothing, and rewards you only for refusing the very
behaviours the rest of the internet is engineered to extract.
