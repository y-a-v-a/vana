Rivers

after Johannes Gutenberg · text-align: justify

This column is set flush to both margins. To square the right edge your browser widens the spaces between the words — never the letters, never the words themselves, only the air. It does this in silence, and it does it again the instant the column changes width. The even block you are reading is an accomplishment of the layout engine; the pale channels drifting down through it are the invoice.

Typographers call those channels rivers and spend whole careers draining them. They are not in the source. Select this paragraph and copy it: every widened gap collapses back to one plain space, because not one of them was ever really there. Drag the handle below and watch the rivers migrate, silt up, dry out. Gutenberg squared his margins by hand — with abbreviations and a drawer of special ligatures. You square yours by declaring two words of style and letting the machine improvise the rest, differently, forever.
