# Hierarchy of Genres

In 1667 André Félibien, secretary to the French Académie, fixed the *hierarchy of genres*: history painting outranked portraiture, which outranked the genre scene, then landscape, then — at the bottom — still life. Centuries later the two most common formats in image-making are still called, in plain English and in every print dialog, **portrait** and **landscape**. The category and the orientation share a name. This work takes that pun literally: it binds the academic rank of the painting to the physical orientation of the rectangle you are holding.

The mechanism is the argument (**P1**). A single read of the viewport's aspect ratio — the most banal line in front-end code, the thing every `@media (orientation: …)` query asks — decides which genre the canvas paints and which rank the wall label reports. Turn a phone upright and you have a Portrait (rank II); turn it sideways and the same hue, the same visit, becomes a Landscape (rank IV); square it off and it sinks to Still Life (rank V). Nothing about the *content* earns the rank; the academy's prestige collapses into an accident of which way gravity happens to point. History painting — rank I — is named on the label but never shown, because it needs figures-in-action this format cannot stage. The top of the hierarchy is structurally out of reach.

It is a **digital readymade** (**P2**): the orientation media query and the four-hundred-year-old genre system are both found objects, fused so that one *is* the other. It targets value and the apparatus that confers it (**P6**) by staging the full institutional kit — artist, medium ("pixels on screen"), format, rank — as a Koons-style wall label whose authority is undone by a wrist-flick. It is double-coded (**P7**, **P9**): a casual viewer gets a clean visual gag (rotate, the picture changes), a literate one gets Félibien and the question of why a still life was ever worth less than a battle scene. And it is the smallest build that completes the thought (**P3**) — no animation, no libraries, one canvas, one resize handler.

It answers Mel Bochner's *Measurement* pieces (which measured the room/viewport as quantity) by measuring it as *taste* instead: not how big, but how prestigious. The hue is minted once per visit with `Math.random` so rotation reframes the same work rather than spawning a new one — provenance held steady while the price tag swings. Open licence, no cookies, no trackers, nothing leaves the device; the only thing being ranked is the angle of your screen.
