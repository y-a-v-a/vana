# Mobile (Solved for Equilibrium)

A hanging mobile in Calder's primary colours is built live in your browser. It is a binary tree of rods and weighted disks, but nothing about its composition is *chosen*: each rod's suspension point is placed by the law of the lever, so that torque balances (`weightₗ · armₗ = weightᵣ · armᵣ`). The artist does not arrange the piece; physics solves it. What varies is thrown fresh on every load — how many parts, their sizes, their colours, the string lengths — and then the browser's animation loop supplies the air, so the thing sways and never resolves into a single picture. Drag across it and you become the draft that moves it.

The mechanism *is* the argument, not decoration (**P1**). The picture you see is literally the equilibrium solution of the parts the page happened to draw — change one disk's weight and every arm above it must reposition. This sits squarely on the chance↔order axis (**P5**): *order* is the deterministic lever mathematics that fixes the geometry; *chance* is the per-visit random parts and the pseudo-turbulent wind that perturbs an equilibrium it must always return to. It is alive rather than fixed (**P4**) — a mobile is defined by refusing to hold one image, which is exactly why it **would die as a print**: a screenshot freezes the one state the work exists to escape. `prefers-reduced-motion` makes that cynical joke land (**P9**): ask for stillness and you are handed the corpse — the print the mobile was never meant to become.

It answers **Alexander Calder** directly, the branch of kinetic sculpture absent from the catalogue. The narrative hook ties back to the DNA's conceptual root: the word *mobile* was coined by **Marcel Duchamp** in 1931 (and *stabile* by **Jean Arp**), so naming this species of object is itself a readymade gesture from the patron saint. It also answers **Kenneth Martin's** *Chance and Order* drawings and y-a-v-a's own **A Chance of Order** (2021) — but where those resolve chance into a static composition, this refuses to settle at all.

Double-coded (**P7**): the casual viewer gets a cheerful, breathing Calder; the literate viewer gets the argument that a mobile has no authored composition and no photograph — only a behaviour of balance. Smallest build that completes the thought (**P3**): SVG, primary colours, black rod and string, no chrome, no libraries, no canvas. Fully self-contained and client-side — no cookies, no storage, no network of any kind — under **CC BY-SA 4.0**, attributed to y-a-v-a and to Calder.
