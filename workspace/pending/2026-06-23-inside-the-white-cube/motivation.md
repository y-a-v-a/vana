# Inside the White Cube

A trivial coloured square is hung in an ordinary webpage — between a header, two
paragraphs of prose, and a licensing footer. It looks like nothing, because it
is nothing. Then you click **Enter the gallery**, the browser's Fullscreen API
fires, and every scrap of context is annihilated: the tabs, the address bar, the
operating system, the rest of the internet. The square does not change a single
pixel. Only its surroundings are deleted — and a caption fades in to confirm the
result: *Now it is art.*

The work answers Brian O'Doherty's *Inside the White Cube: The Ideology of the
Gallery Space* (1976), which argued that the windowless modern gallery is never
neutral: by erasing the world around an object it manufactures that object's aura
and value. The wall does the work. It also sits in the lineage of Duchamp's
readymade (**P2**, digital readymade): what gets signed is not the object but the
*context* — here, a generic browser rectangle recontextualised into a precious
monochrome by an act of framing.

The mechanism *is* the argument (**P1**): fullscreen is the browser's own white
cube, and calling it performs exactly O'Doherty's operation — stripping all
worldly context to sacralise what remains. Nothing is added to the square; the
apparatus does everything, which is precisely the critique. The piece is
double-coded (**P7**): the surface gag is "the gallery is just fullscreen," while
the literate reading is the institutional-critique claim that value lives in the
frame, not the framed (**P6**, value/authenticity). If the browser refuses the
request, a faux cube admits as much — even the white cube is *granted* by an
apparatus that can say no, which only sharpens the point.

It is the smallest build that completes the thought (**P3**): one DOM toggle, one
API call, no libraries, no assets. The square is freshly hued by `Math.random`
on every load, so the work is live and per-visit rather than a fixed print
(**P4**). It could not exist as a printed object — its entire subject is the
deletion of context that only a screen's chrome can perform (**G1**). Open
licence stated, attribution to O'Doherty and Duchamp given, and nothing leaves
the browser: no cookies, no trackers, no storage, no network (**G4**).
