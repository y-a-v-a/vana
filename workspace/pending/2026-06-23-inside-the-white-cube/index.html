Inside the White Cube

A monochrome, hung in a webpage.

Untitled · pixels on screen
Here it is just a coloured square in a browser tab, surrounded by a header, some prose, and a footer.

(The gallery is just fullscreen.)

Now it is art.

Esc to leave the gallery

Brian O’Doherty argued that the white cube — the windowless, contextless modern gallery — is not neutral at all. By deleting the world around the object, it manufactures the object’s value and aura. The wall does the work.

The browser has its own white cube: fullscreen. One call and the tabs, the address bar, the operating system, the rest of the internet all vanish. The square does not change. Its surroundings are deleted, and that — apparently — is enough.

Inside the White Cube (2026) — y-a-v-a.
After Brian O’Doherty, Inside the White Cube: The Ideology of the Gallery Space (1976). In the lineage of the readymade (Marcel Duchamp): context, not the object, is what is signed.
