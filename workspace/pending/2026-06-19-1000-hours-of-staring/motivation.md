# 1000 Hours of Staring

Tom Friedman's *1000 Hours of Staring* (1992–1997) is a sheet of blank paper that the artist claims to have stared at for a thousand hours over five years. There is nothing to see and nothing to verify; the work is pure invisible labour, sold and collected on the strength of a sentence. This page restages that exactly. A near-blank sheet hangs on a gallery-grey wall, and a clock accumulates *your* staring time toward the same thousand hours.

The mechanism is the argument (**P1**, **G3**). The browser ships a precise instrument for "is anyone actually looking at this": `document.visibilityState` and `document.hasFocus()`. The clock advances **only** while the tab is foreground and focused, and stops the instant you switch away — attention itself is the medium, gated by the platform's own attention APIs. But the page is honest about its blind spot: focus is not looking. It cannot tell whether your eyes are on the sheet, only that you have not left. That gap is the whole point — the unverifiability Friedman's collectors had to swallow on faith is now a literal property of the code (**P6**, value/authenticity).

The total lives in `localStorage` and nowhere else. It is private, never transmitted, and therefore has no provenance, no edition, and nothing to auction — the running line reads "unverifiable, unsellable, yours." Only the web could stage labour this way: a thing that exists solely as accumulated, discardable attention you cannot prove and cannot sell (**G1**). It would die instantly as a print, which would simply be a blank sheet again.

It answers Friedman directly, and sits beside the catalogue's other time-as-material works — *Today* (On Kawara) and *1965 / 1 – ∞* (Opałka) — by giving their counting an **attention gate and a market joke**: here time only counts when watched, and counting it is worth nothing. On the chance↔order axis (**P5**) it is pure order — a strict accumulator — undone by the chance of where you point your eyes. The wit is dry (**P9**): "≈ 41 days of unbroken attention remain" is an invitation no one will accept, which is exactly the cheerful-cynic note.
