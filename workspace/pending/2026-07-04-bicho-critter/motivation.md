# Bicho (Critter)

A *Bicho* — Portuguese for "beast" or "critter" — is a small articulated creature made of hinged planes. It has no front, no back, no top, no privileged pose. On load, one is minted at random: a different number of plates, folded at random angles, floating with no ground beneath it. It sits perfectly still. It has no idle animation, no timer, no drift — it does absolutely nothing until you take hold of a plane and fold it, and it stops the instant you let go. Reload and the animal is gone: a new one takes its place, and there is no way back to the last one, because there never was an original.

The work answers **Lygia Clark's *Bicho* (1960)** and the **Neo-Concrete Manifesto (Ferreira Gullar, 1959)**, which broke with the Constructivist / kinetic idea of the artwork-as-machine and insisted the piece is a *quasi-corpus* — a near-living body that only becomes itself in the hand of the participant. Clark said the Bicho "is a living organism, a work essentially active"; it exists in the relation, not as an object on a plinth. It also answers **Duchamp's "The Creative Act" (1957)**: the spectator completes the work. And it sits against **Walter Benjamin** — a Bicho you fold is unique and unrepeatable, so there is no authentic image to reproduce.

The **mechanism is the argument, not decoration** (P1). The whole thing is a forward-kinematic linkage: plates chained by hinges, motion generated *only* by `pointerdown`/`pointermove` and killed on release — no `requestAnimationFrame`, no autoplay, no life without the participant. That absence is the Neo-Concrete claim rendered literally in code: the object is inert; the art is the relation you perform with it. The refusal of persistence is the same argument continued — no storage, no cookies, no network, and no reset button, because a reset implies a canonical state to return to, and a Bicho has none. Each load's random genesis (P5, chance) folded through a rigid articulated linkage (P5, order) makes every visit an unrepeatable animal.

It reads at two depths (P7): a casual visitor gets a foldable metal critter that obeys the cursor; a literate one gets a phenomenological object with no correct form, no original, and no existence apart from being handled — dry, deadpan, and completed by the hand (P3, P4, P9). Web/AI-native (G1): it cannot be a print, because a print is fixed, singular, and does exactly the one thing this work refuses — hold still in one authored pose.
