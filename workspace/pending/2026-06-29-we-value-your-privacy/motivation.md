# We Value Your Privacy

The most-clicked interface element on the modern web is not a link or a play button — it is the cookie-consent banner, the "Accept All" that hundreds of millions of people dismiss every day without reading. This work takes that banner as a digital readymade (**P2**) and installs it whole, in its native plumbing: a real `<dialog>` element opened with `showModal()`, the same component a compliance vendor would ship. Nothing is parodied at the level of style; the form is presented as found. The only difference is that here the apparatus governs nothing, because there is nothing to govern.

The concept *is* the execution (**P1**). The page genuinely sets no cookies, writes no storage, and makes no network request — so the work audits itself, live, with the browser's own instruments: `document.cookie`, `localStorage.length`, `sessionStorage.length`, and `performance.getEntriesByType('resource')`. Whatever you click — Accept All, Reject All, flip every toggle in "Manage Preferences" — the readout stays at zero, and the banner cheerfully announces "Your preferences have been saved" over an audit that proves nothing was saved. The mechanism doesn't illustrate the argument about consent theatre; it *is* the consent theatre, running honestly, with the meter exposed. The delivery ethics (**P8**) are not a disclaimer at the bottom — they are the artwork's whole content.

It answers Hans Haacke's institutional critique — works like *MoMA Poll* (1970), where the gallery's own participatory apparatus (the ballot, the questionnaire) is turned back on the institution as the medium. Here the institution is the regulated commercial web, and its defining participatory ritual is the consent dialog: a vote with a single, pre-counted outcome. Behind Haacke stands Duchamp's readymade — an object lifted intact from the system that produced it, recontextualised by a title and a label. The reference at the surface is dry and current (the GDPR / ePrivacy banner everyone resents); the reference underneath is a fifty-year-old argument about who the apparatus actually serves.

On the chance/order axis (**P5**) this sits at the pure-order pole: a deterministic dead control, identical in every browser, with no randomness and no entropy — its rigidity is the point. And it lands the value/authenticity target (**P6**) by reading the banner's central lie literally: "We value your privacy" is a *value* claim, and the live audit is the appraisal. The valuation comes back at zero in every column, which is, deadpan, exactly correct.
