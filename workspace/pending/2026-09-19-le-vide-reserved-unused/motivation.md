# Le Vide (Reserved, Unused)

In 1958 Yves Klein emptied the Galerie Iris Clert, painted it white, and called the emptiness the work. Three thousand people queued to stand in a reserved, immaterial room that held nothing but its own allotted sensibility. This piece finds the same reserved emptiness already sitting on your device: every web origin is granted an enormous private storage quota — commonly gigabytes — a room the browser sets aside in case the site wants to fill it. This artwork declines. It writes nothing, and then it measures, live, the exact dimensions of the space it is keeping empty.

The mechanism is the argument (**P1**). The work does not *depict* a void; it queries `navigator.storage.estimate()` and renders the browser's own numbers — a quota of billions of bytes, a usage of zero — as a to-scale bar in which the "used" portion is a nonexistent sliver drowning in reserved white. Concept and execution are one object: the emptiness on screen *is* the emptiness the API reports, and the API can only report zero because the page genuinely stores nothing. The DNA's non-negotiable delivery ethic (**P8** — no cookies, no storage, no trackers) stops being a footnote and becomes the medium: the ethic is the artwork, and it is falsifiable — re-measure and the counter must still read 0.

It answers Klein's *Le Vide* directly (**P2**, digital readymade — the storage quota is the appropriated cultural object), and stands beside the dematerialization lineage of Robert Barry's *During the exhibition the gallery will be closed* and the museum-depot works, but relocates the void from the gallery to the machine's file system. The novelty (**P4**, **P6**) is one of *location and ontology*: a new place for an artwork to live — an allocated-but-unwritten quota — and a fresh answer to "but is it art / but what is it worth," priced not in dollars but in reserved bytes deliberately left blank. On the chance↔order axis this sits at pure order: nothing is generated, nothing drifts, the only number that moves is your device's bookkeeping.

Double-coded (**P7**): the casual visitor reads a dry joke — the browser hands this page gigabytes and it uses none of them, "reserved for nothing, which is the work." The literate visitor reads Klein's whitewashed void, Benjamin's aura relocated into the immaterial, and the quiet cheerful-cynic point (**P9**) that the most honest thing a website can offer in the surveillance economy is a measurable, verifiable *nothing stored*. The smallest build that completes the thought (**P3**): one API call, one bar, one wall label.

**References answered:** Yves Klein, *Le Vide* (1958); Robert Barry, *During the exhibition the gallery will be closed* (1969); Walter Benjamin, *The Work of Art in the Age of Mechanical Reproduction* (1935); the browser `StorageManager` quota (net.art — device bookkeeping as material).
