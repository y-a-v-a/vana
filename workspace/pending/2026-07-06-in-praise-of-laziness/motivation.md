# In Praise of Laziness

A field of graphite strokes accumulates on a canvas — but only in the moments the browser has nothing to do. The work is painted inside `requestIdleCallback`, the scheduling hook browsers offer so developers can squeeze useful work out of a machine's spare cycles. Here that productivity engine is turned exactly inside out: each stroke is drawn precisely as long as the idle time the browser confesses it has to give (`deadline.timeRemaining()`), and callbacks forced to run with no idle budget (`deadline.didTimeout`) add nothing at all. Scroll, click or type and the main thread is put back to work, the idle callbacks starve, and the painting stops growing. Leave the tab entirely alone and it fills. Neglect makes the picture; attention kills it.

The mechanism *is* the argument (**P1**): the piece is not a picture *about* laziness, it is a picture that can only be produced *by* laziness — the browser's own scheduler is the readymade (**P2**), repurposed from harvesting spare cycles to squandering them beautifully. It lives as a behaviour rather than an artifact, regenerated per visit and wiped on reload (**P4**), and it sits on the chance/order axis (**P5**): the *timing* of every stroke is handed over to the non-deterministic idle scheduler (chance), while the strokes lay down in strict left-to-right, row-by-row order. The surface gag reads instantly — a painting that only grows while you do nothing — and rewards a second, drier look at the whole attention economy the code politely refuses to join (**P7, P9**). It collects nothing, sends nothing, stores nothing (**P8**); a work that thrives on inattention is the structural opposite of engagement-farming.

It answers Mladen Stilinović's *In Praise of Laziness* (1993) — "There is no art without laziness" — by taking the claim literally at the level of the CPU, and nods to Lee Lozano's *General Strike Piece* (1969) and to Duchamp's long chess-playing withdrawal from making. Where the earlier y-a-v-a catalogue measured attention as an unsellable medium (staring, presence, following), this measures its absence: the painting is a live ledger of your device's idleness, and the one browser that refuses to admit it is ever idle is honestly told the work cannot be made for it.

The build is the smallest that completes the thought (**P3**): one canvas, one idle loop, a Haacke-style data plate confessing the numbers, and a wall label. No frameworks, no assets, no requests.
