# In Praise of Laziness

A field of graphite strokes accumulates on a canvas — but only in the moments when two kinds of idleness coincide: the browser's and yours. The work is painted inside `requestIdleCallback`, the scheduling hook browsers offer so developers can squeeze useful work out of a machine's spare cycles. Here that productivity engine is turned exactly inside out: each stroke is drawn precisely as long as the idle time the browser confesses it has to give (`deadline.timeRemaining()`), and callbacks forced to run with no idle budget (`deadline.didTimeout`) add nothing at all. But the stroke is now also gated on the viewer's own stillness: the page watches for `mousemove`, `scroll`, `keydown` and their kin, and paints only when your hand has lain untouched for 400 ms. Move the mouse and the stillness breaks — the painting stops mid-stroke; go quiet again and it resumes. Neglect makes the picture; the smallest stir kills it.

The mechanism *is* the argument (**P1**): the piece is not a picture *about* laziness, it is a picture that can only be produced *by* laziness — and now laziness is required at both ends of the glass, machine and hand. The browser's own scheduler is the readymade (**P2**), repurposed from harvesting spare cycles to squandering them beautifully, while the input listeners — the very sensors an engagement economy uses to prove you are *active* — are inverted to reward your doing absolutely nothing. It lives as a behaviour rather than an artifact, regenerated per visit and wiped on reload (**P4**), and it sits on the chance/order axis (**P5**): the *timing* of every stroke is handed over to the non-deterministic idle scheduler (chance), while the strokes lay down in strict left-to-right, row-by-row order. The surface gag reads instantly — a painting that only grows while you sit perfectly still — and rewards a second, drier look at the whole attention economy the code politely refuses to join (**P7, P9**). It collects nothing, sends nothing, stores nothing (**P8**); the mouse is watched only to be ignored, and a work that thrives on inattention is the structural opposite of engagement-farming.

It answers Mladen Stilinović's *In Praise of Laziness* (1993) — "There is no art without laziness" — by taking the claim literally at the level of both the CPU and the viewer's own hand, and nods to Lee Lozano's *General Strike Piece* (1969) and to Duchamp's long chess-playing withdrawal from making. Where the earlier y-a-v-a catalogue measured attention as an unsellable medium (staring, presence, following), this measures its absence: the painting is a live ledger of your stillness, and the one browser that refuses to admit it is ever idle is honestly told the work cannot be made for it.

The build is the smallest that completes the thought (**P3**): one canvas, one idle loop, a Haacke-style data plate confessing the numbers, and a wall label. No frameworks, no assets, no requests.
