Non-finito

after Michelangelo's Prigioni — figures the sculptor left inside the stone

The figure was always inside the stone; the sculptor removes only what was never the figure, and leaves the rest.

Every statement about art is a block the reader is asked to finish, because the machine releases exactly as much as fits.

Non-finito is not a failure of the hand but a wager that the work is most alive at the instant it decides to stop.

You are reading the width of your own screen, not my sentence, since the browser carved this line to the marble you brought.

The mark at the edge is the browser's chisel: it belongs to no author, appears in no source, and vanishes when you copy.

Widen the window and more of me emerges; narrow it and I return to the stone, half-born, awaiting a viewport that finishes.
