# Perfect Lovers (Reset)

Two identical wall clocks hang side by side, cases touching, set to exactly the same time. This is Félix González-Torres's *"Untitled" (Perfect Lovers)* (1991): a portrait of two lovers as two commercial clocks, made while his partner Ross was dying of AIDS. The clocks start in perfect synchrony and, because no two batteries deplete at the same rate, drift apart — until one runs slow, then stops. The certificate instructs that they be reset to the same time and restarted. The work is the inevitability of the drift, not the clocks.

This page advances the two clocks from a single starting time read once, locally, from your machine. Each is then driven by its own slightly imperfect rate and its own draining battery — one lover runs a hair slow and carries the weaker cell. The page never resyncs to the real clock and never corrects them against each other; the gap only widens, and the second hands you saw touching come apart on their own. When a battery crosses its final eighth the hand slows, the way real clocks die, and then it halts. The only way they are ever set to the same time again is a reload — which is exactly the curator's gesture in the certificate, and exactly the futile, loving act of resetting a thing that will only drift again.

The mechanism *is* the elegy (**P1**). The argument is not painted onto the clocks; it is the JS timer model — independent rates plus battery depletion plus a refusal to resync — that produces the separation. Nothing about it can be a static print: a print fixes one instant, and the whole point is the unfixable widening over time, live, per visit (**G1**, **P4**). It sits squarely on the chance/order axis (**P5**): two clocks are pure order, the simplest of mechanisms, and entropy — drift, depletion, the dying battery — is the chance that pulls order apart. The reload-as-reset is the web-native readymade gesture (**P2**): the platform already contains the certificate's instruction.

It reads at two depths (**P7**). A casual viewer sees two matched clocks slowly, quietly going out of step, one eventually stopping — a small sadness needing no wall text. A literate viewer gets González-Torres, the portrait, the AIDS elegy, and the certificate that asks the impossible: set them to the same time again. The voice is the dry cheerful cynic (**P9**): the instruction line — *"They will not be set again until you reload"* — states the consolation and its emptiness in the same breath.

Delivered self-contained, client-side only, CC BY-NC, no cookies, no trackers, attributed to y-a-v-a and to González-Torres (**P8**, **G4**). It is materially distinct from every catalogued work: not a counter (*1965 / 1 – ∞*), not a date (*Today*), not a render toggle (*The webpage going on and off*) — but two synchronized things and the entropy that separates them.
