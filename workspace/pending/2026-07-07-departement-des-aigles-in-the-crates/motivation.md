# Département des Aigles (In the Crates)

A museum of twelve eagles in which nothing is on view. The walls are empty frames, each naming a *Fig.* and denying, in Broodthaers' own words, that it is art. The works themselves — twelve small SVG eagles — are really present in the page: parsed, catalogued, measurable. They live inside HTML `<template>` elements, and by the specification the contents of a `<template>` are parsed into an inert document fragment that the browser **never renders**. The collection is complete and completely unexhibited. The crate is the medium.

This answers Marcel Broodthaers' *Musée d'Art Moderne, Département des Aigles, Section des Figures* (1968–72): a fictional museum, administratively real, whose objects were mundane eagles labelled *"Ceci n'est pas un objet d'art"* — a wall label borrowed straight from René Magritte's *The Treachery of Images*. It also answers the plain institutional fact that museums keep the overwhelming majority of what they own in storage, permanently out of sight. The `<template>` tag is the DOM's own vault.

The mechanism *is* the argument (**P1**, **P3**). I am not drawing an empty gallery to *illustrate* absence; the browser genuinely will not render what is inside a `<template>`. The count is honest and derived, not decorative: "held" reads the number of crates, "on view" counts rendered `<svg>` on the walls — structurally zero. "Open the crates" then does what a registrar does and a curator does not: it reaches into each inert fragment, confirms the eagle is a real node, reports its markup and dimensions to the register — and hangs nothing. Presence without exhibition. The tally stays at zero because opening a crate is not the same as putting a work on the wall.

This is a **digital readymade** (**P2**): the appropriated cultural object is a browser behaviour whose literal operation — a parsed, present, non-rendered fragment — enacts the vaulted, catalogued, invisible collection. It sits on the *order* pole of the chance/order axis (**P5**): an institution, a register, a numbered typology of near-identical figures (the eagles vary only by rotation and tone, in the Becher-typology spirit). It is double-coded (**P7**): a casual visitor reads an empty museum whose label insists the missing pictures aren't art; a literate one reads Broodthaers, Magritte, and the exact spec clause that makes `<template>` content inert. The tone is the dry museum registrar (**P9**) — administratively certain that the eagles are here, and just as certain they will not be shown.

Delivery honours the ethic (**P8**): one self-contained page, no network, no cookies, no trackers, no storage, licensed CC BY 4.0, attributed to y-a-v-a after Broodthaers (and Magritte's label).
