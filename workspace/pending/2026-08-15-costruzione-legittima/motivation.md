# Costruzione Legittima

In 1435 Leon Battista Alberti wrote down the *costruzione legittima* — the "legitimate construction" of linear perspective that Brunelleschi had demonstrated with his mirror and his painted Baptistery. Its founding fiction is a single, sovereign viewpoint: the picture is "an open window" onto a world that obediently converges to one point, the eye of one standing observer. Five centuries later the browser ships that exact fiction as a declaration. The CSS `perspective` property *is* the distance from Alberti's eye to his picture plane, measured in pixels; `perspective-origin` *is* the vanishing point. This work is nothing but those two values, held live.

The mechanism is the argument (**P1**). I did not draw a floor in perspective — I laid a flat, self-identical checkerboard (Alberti's *pavimento*) on the page and let the browser's projection engine make it recede. The convergence you see is not painted; it is computed each frame from `perspective` and `perspective-origin`, the same maths the *Della Pittura* prescribes. That makes it a digital readymade (**P2**) of a browser behaviour, and a piece of pure *order* on the chance/order axis (**P5**): rule-based projective geometry, with only a per-visit hue left to chance.

The twist — the cheerful-cynic idea underneath (**P7**, **P9**) — is that perspective sold itself as *the* truthful, God-given way to see, one authoritative station point. But here the vanishing point is wherever your cursor happens to be. Slide left and the whole architecture of the world re-obeys a different centre; lift the eye far away and depth flattens back into the medieval plane it came from; lean in and the floor plunges. The "legitimate construction" turns out to be a slider. Naming the live values in the corner (`perspective: 480px`) keeps the readymade honest: you are watching a doctrine reduced to a property. It could not be a print (**G1**) — a print fixes exactly the one viewpoint this piece refuses to fix. It answers Alberti and Brunelleschi directly, and sits beside the practice's other measurement works (Bochner) as their pre-history: the first time a flat surface was told to pretend it had a depth and an owner.

Restraint holds it to the smallest build that completes the thought (**P3**): one scene, one floor, two CSS custom properties, a pointer handler, no libraries, no network, no storage. Open licence stated on the face of the work (**P8**).
