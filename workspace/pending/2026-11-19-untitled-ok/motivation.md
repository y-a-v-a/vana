**Untitled (OK)** is a white page with a running clock and a red bar that says *Press to be addressed*. Pressing it calls `window.alert()`: a second-person command in capitals ("NOTHING ON THIS PAGE MOVES UNTIL YOU AGREE.") appears in a box drawn by the operating system, with one button. While the box is open the clock stops, because the whole page has stopped. When you click OK, the page measures how long it was halted, counts the frames it painted in that time (always 0) and records your reply (always OK).

The reference is Barbara Kruger: the imperative "you" of advertising, set in white type on a red bar (*Untitled (Your gaze hits the side of my face)*, 1981; *Untitled (I shop therefore I am)*, 1987). The web already has its own version of that voice, and it is cruder than Kruger's. `alert()` is the only HTML element that freezes the JavaScript thread and the render loop until you answer, and the only answer it accepts is consent. Kruger had to design her red bar. The alert box can't be styled at all: the browser vendor sets the typeface and decides the layout. The work borrows Kruger's voice in a frame that refuses her look (P2, digital readymade). It also answers y-a-v-a's *The Agreeing Internet* (2009). There the site agreed with you. Here you have to agree with the site.

The mechanism is the argument (P1, G3). "Nothing moves until you agree" is literally true: the stopped clock, the honest `frames painted: 0` and the measured halt are not illustrations of power. They are what the call does. The second level of the work (P7) is about refusal. Neither the page nor the artist offers a way to say no. After a few dialogs, some browsers offer a checkbox to *prevent this page from creating additional dialogs*. If you tick it, `alert()` returns in about 0 ms and the ledger reports that the work has been silenced. So the only way to dissent is something the vendor grants, and the artist can't take it away. That is the cheerful-cynic punchline about who actually owns the imperative voice on the web (P9, P6 transposed from market to platform power).

The work is live and per-visit (P4). It uses no storage, cookies or network, and the tally lives in RAM until reload (P8). The build is about 60 lines (P3). On the chance/order axis (P5) it sits at the order end: a strict queue of commands answered in one fixed way. The only chance left is the length of your hesitation, and the ledger records it to the millisecond.
