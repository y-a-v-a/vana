# Ganzfeld (Esc to Leave)

A single edgeless field of light. Click *Enter the field* and the browser takes your cursor and, from that moment, tells the page only how far the mouse has moved — `movementX`, `movementY` — never where it is. There is no coordinate, no pointer, no border. You can travel tens of thousands of pixels and arrive nowhere, because the medium has withdrawn the one thing that would let you locate yourself. This is exactly James Turrell's *Ganzfeld*: a homogeneous perceptual field in which the eye, starved of edges, loses depth, loses the sense of a surface, and begins to invent shimmer and drift that are not in the room. Here that drift is honest — the pixels never change; whatever you see move happens in your visual cortex.

The work is web/AI-native and could not survive as a print (G1): its entire subject is the **Pointer Lock API** (net.art material), whose literal, security-motivated behaviour — *hide the cursor, report relative motion, forbid absolute position* — **is** the Ganzfeld (P1, concept is execution). Nothing is illustrated. The dissolution of the index is not depicted; it is performed by the browser refusing to tell you where you are. It sits on the order↔chance axis (P5): a strict rule (lock → no position, no edge) let loose on the viewer's chance wandering, over a per-visit luminous hue chosen by `Math.random`.

It answers **James Turrell** (the *Ganzfeld* series, e.g. *Ganzfeld: APANI*, 2011, and the *Perceptual Cells*), and behind him **Robert Irwin's** phrase *"seeing yourself see"* and **Yves Klein's** *Le Vide* (1958) — the empty gallery crowds queued to see nothing. The twenty-year argument holds: a canonical perceptual-art gesture turns out to already live inside the browser, as an accident of a feature built for first-person shooters and anti-cheating.

The final turn is the mechanism, not the wall text (P3, P7, P9). The one way out of the sublime void is **Esc**, and the browser keeps that key for itself — you cannot disable it, and it will yank the cursor back on the smallest loss of focus. The void is real but you are not permitted to stay; the readout counts your distance and reports your position as `—` the entire time, then resets to the same field on re-entry. Cheerful cynic, dry: an infinite, edgeless sublime that the browser politely refuses to let you keep. No cookies, no network, nothing recorded (P8); CC BY-SA 4.0, y-a-v-a, after James Turrell.
