# The Original

A museum certificate of authenticity, rendered as a webpage. It declares itself *Edition 1 of 1* and offers a single button: **Acquire the original**. Clicking it downloads `the-original.html` — a faithful copy of the very page you are reading — and quietly increments a counter that now reports you are one of *N* owners of the only one. The certificate is real. It is also self-issued, browser-local, and therefore worth exactly nothing.

The work answers Walter Benjamin's *The Work of Art in the Age of Mechanical Reproduction* (1935) by relocating his argument to the one medium where it is no longer a thesis but a fact. On the web there is no original and no copy, only perfect, costless reproduction; the "aura" of the unique object cannot survive a context where every visitor already holds a byte-faithful instance. The page also stands in the lineage of the digital readymade (Duchamp) and net.art: the apparatus of the gallery — the wall label, the edition number, the signed certificate — is the found object being recontextualised (**P2**).

The mechanism is the argument, not its illustration (**P1, G3**). Authenticity here is enacted by `Blob` + a download attribute: the act of "buying the original" *produces* the original, identical for everyone, infinitely. Scarcity is enacted by a single `localStorage` integer — private, unsynced, trivially editable. The page even invites you to "view source to forge your own provenance," because the only thing standing between you and certified ownership is a number you can overwrite in the console. Value, fame and authenticity are the standing targets (**P6**); the piece sits on the *order* side of the chance/order axis — a deterministic system whose every output is identical by design (**P5**).

It is double-coded (**P7**): the casual visitor gets a dry joke — "1 of 1" contradicted in the same breath by "issued from this browser: 7" — while the literate visitor gets a working demonstration of Benjamin's lost aura, and a jab at the certificate-of-authenticity economy that the NFT era tried to bolt back onto infinitely copyable files. It is the smallest build that completes the thought (**P3**): one page, one button, one counter, no server, no external request, no tracker. The work cannot exist as a print — a print cannot hand you itself (**G1**).
