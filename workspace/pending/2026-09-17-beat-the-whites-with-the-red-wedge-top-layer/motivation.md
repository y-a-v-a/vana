# Beat the Whites with the Red Wedge (Top Layer)

El Lissitzky's 1919 lithograph *Klinom krasnym bei belykh* — *Beat the Whites with the Red Wedge* — is a slogan made geometry: a sharp red triangle drives into a white circle, the Reds splitting the Whites of the civil war. His slightly later *Proun* works push the same forms into a space with no gravity and no fixed up or down — "the interchange station between painting and architecture." This piece hands both to the browser. A red wedge stands on a white field; press **Raise a white** and translucent sheets fall over the whole viewport at `z-index: 2147483647` — the single largest integer CSS will accept — burying the wedge. Press **Affirm to the top layer** and the same wedge leaps out of the document into the browser's *top layer* (via the HTML Popover API), where it beats the whites.

The mechanism is the slogan, not an illustration of it (P1). The top layer is a real, recent web-platform construct: an element promoted there renders above **every** stacking context and ignores `z-index` entirely — you cannot out-stack it, because it is no longer playing the stacking game. So the demonstration is honest and unwinnable in the document: pile the whites as high as the integer type allows and they still lose the instant the wedge is affirmed. "Beat the Whites with the Red Wedge" becomes a literal statement about CSS layering. This is a digital readymade of a platform behaviour (P2), and the wedge's escape from the document's gravity into a groundless top layer echoes the weightless *Proun* space (P7 — a clean visual gag over a precise art-historical and rendering-engine argument).

The composition is rule-based but re-thrown on every visit — wedge angle, circle, beam, and the small black Suprematist square vary within Constructivist constraints (P5, chance within order), so no two loads carry the same drawing and nothing is stored to make one repeatable. It answers Lissitzky and, behind him, his teacher Malevich, whom this catalogue keeps orbiting; it sits beside the collection's other "browser chrome as material" works (the scrollbar-as-zip, the favicon-as-Mondrian) but takes a mechanism none of them use: the top layer as the plane above the document's order.

The wit is a carrier wave (P9): a small, faintly absurd z-index arms race that the document is built to lose, dry rather than triumphal. It is the smallest build that completes the thought (P3) — one SVG form, a stack of translucent sheets, one call to `showPopover()`. It lives only in the browser: no network, no cookies, no trackers, no storage; it is licensed CC BY-SA 4.0 in keeping with its own Constructivist, collective source, and attributed to y-a-v-a after El Lissitzky.
