Pierced Form

In 1931 Barbara Hepworth bored a hole clean through a block of alabaster and decided the hole was the sculpture. Everything solid around it had become mere support — stone kept on only to give the opening an edge. What you are meant to look at is the part that was taken away. Here there is no stone to take away. The opening below is not drawn; it is only declared, a region the layout engine has been told the words may not occupy. The sentences arrive, meet the boundary of a shape that has no colour, no border, no pixels of its own, and turn aside. The void you see is made entirely of their refusal to enter it. Carve with the control: widen the aperture and the text retreats further, a revelation performed by subtraction. Close it to nothing and the block heals into uncarved prose, the sculpture un-made. The opening keeps no record of itself and reflows with every window; where each word happens to break around the curve is left to chance and to your device. The stone is not shown because, on the web, there was never any stone — only the hole, and the paragraph bending around the place where it is not.
