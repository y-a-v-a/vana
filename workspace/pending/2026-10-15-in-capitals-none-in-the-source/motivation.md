# In Capitals (None in the Source)

Capital letters are the typography of permanence. The Roman inscription, the lapidary frieze on the museum pediment, the donor's name endowed *in perpetuity*, the all-caps authority of the headline and the monument — majuscules are how a culture says *this will outlast you*. This work takes that claim literally and then checks it. The inscription you read is set entirely in grand carved capitals; the text in the document is entirely lowercase. The capitals are supplied by a single CSS declaration, `text-transform: uppercase`, at render time. They exist in the light of the screen and nowhere else.

The mechanism **is** the argument (P1, G3). `text-transform` is a pure presentation effect: the DOM, the Selection API and the clipboard all return the underlying text, which is lowercase. So when the viewer obeys the engraved command "you may take nothing from this room" and copies the inscription, they *do* take it with them — downgraded to a whisper, every monumental capital stripped away. A live ledger attests it: zero capital letters in the source, zero capitals carried off on copy. An "un-chisel" button toggles the one declaration on and off, proving the carving is reversible in a keystroke — permanence as a property you can flip. Nothing here is decoration; remove the mechanism and there is no work, only lowercase text (P3).

It is a **digital readymade** of the web's own typography pipeline (P2) and a direct answer to Horace's boast in *Odes* III.30 — *exegi monumentum aere perennius*, "I have built a monument more lasting than bronze" — and to the Roman monumental capital of Trajan's Column, the ancestor of every carved authority-letter. Against Walter Benjamin's *The Work of Art in the Age of Mechanical Reproduction* (1935): the aura of the inscribed, immovable, unique monument turns out to be the most trivially reproducible thing on the page — except that what reproduces is the mute lowercase substance, never the capitalised authority. It extends the y-a-v-a source-vs-render lineage (*Silencio*, *La Disparition*) to a target those works never touched: letter **case** rather than whitespace or missing glyphs.

The point under the joke is value and authenticity (P6, P7, P9). Monumentality, the endowment "in perpetuity," the capital-C Collection, the capital letters of the market's own self-regard — all of it sits in the render layer, not the record. The viewer gets the gag at a glance (a stone monument that brags it cannot be copied, copied in one click) and the literate viewer gets the argument (presentation masquerading as substance, aura as a CSS pass). Dry, cheerful cynic: the grandest claim on the page is the one that survives reproduction least well.
