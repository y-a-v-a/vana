# Wrapped (Closed Shadow)

A monument stands in the middle of the page, wrapped in silvery cloth and cinched with blue rope. You can see its whole form. You cannot open it — and not because a script forbids you. The wrapping is a `mode:'closed'` Shadow DOM: a real boundary the browser builds into the platform. The document's own JavaScript asks for the canvas inside and gets `null`; asks the host for its `shadowRoot` and gets `null`; a light-DOM CSS rule tries to gut everything inside the work and is silently refused. The fabric is not a picture of a seal. It *is* the seal.

This is a digital readymade (**P2**): Shadow DOM is a piece of ordinary browser plumbing meant to encapsulate widgets, appropriated here as Christo and Jeanne-Claude's cloth. Concept and execution are one object (**P1**) — the argument of the work ("wrapping reveals the form by withholding the surface") is not illustrated by the code, it is performed by the encapsulation itself. The build is the smallest that completes the thought (**P3**): one custom element, one closed root, one canvas, and a panel that fails on cue. The drape is re-cut by chance on every visit and kept only in RAM (**P4**) — like the real installations, it comes down and is never repeated.

It answers **Christo and Jeanne-Claude** directly (*The Pont Neuf Wrapped*, 1985; *Wrapped Reichstag*, 1995; *L'Arc de Triomphe, Wrapped*, 2021), whose wrappings made familiar structures newly visible precisely by denying you their surface. It also turns a fact of the medium against itself: the web is the great "view-source" machine, where everything is copyable and inspectable — yet here is a region the page genuinely cannot read. That is the double coding (**P7**): a casual viewer gets a wrapped landmark; a literate one gets encapsulation staged as concealment, and the quiet cynic's note that only the browser's own developer tools may still lift the cloth — the authorities always keep a key.

The delivery is the ethic (**P8**): CC BY-NC 4.0 stated on the page, no cookies, no trackers, no storage, no network of any kind; the only thing withheld from you is withheld by the browser, in the open, and named as such.
