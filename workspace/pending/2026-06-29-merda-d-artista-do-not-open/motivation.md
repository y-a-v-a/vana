# Merda d'artista (Do Not Open)

In 1961 Piero Manzoni canned thirty grams of his own excrement into ninety signed, numbered tins and priced each one at the day's value of its weight in gold. The joke that is also a thesis: the market does not buy the contents, it buys the signature and the seal. The contents are unverifiable — and must stay that way, because the only way to confirm the artist's claim is to open the tin, which destroys both the seal and the worth. Authenticity and value live entirely in the unopened state. (At least one collector has opened theirs; the tin became, in art-market terms, worth nothing.)

The web already has a widget whose single, literal job is *to reveal hidden contents*: the native `<details>`/`<summary>` disclosure. So the tin **is** a `<details>` element. Closed, it is sealed and ticks a (locally simulated) spot-gold valuation, "payable by weight." The browser's `toggle` event — the moment you open it to check what the artist actually canned — fires `destroy()`: the price strikes through to `$0.00`, irreversibly for this visit, and the contents resolve to "unverifiable. They always were. That was the price." This is **P1 (concept *is* execution)**: I am not illustrating Manzoni's paradox, I am handing the viewer the disclosure mechanism and letting their click perform it. It is a **digital readymade (P2)** of a 1961 readymade, and the smallest build that completes the thought (**P3**): one `<details>`, one event listener.

The recurring target is value and authenticity (**P6**) — staged literally with a gold-pegged price ticker, an edition number (each visitor draws a different tin from the ninety via `Math.random`), and the verdict "authenticity confirmed by destruction." The price's small random walk is the chance engine (**P5**) standing in for "the market, performed," and the gag reads at two depths (**P7**): a casual viewer gets *don't open the poop can, it's worth gold*; a literate one gets Manzoni's attack on the cult of the artist's signature and Duchamp's *infrathin* — the immeasurably thin difference between the sealed thing and the same thing known. Humor carries the idea, not the other way round (**P9**).

The mechanism is the argument because the value is held in a state, not in a substance: it exists only while the disclosure stays closed, and the browser collapses it the instant curiosity wins. No image of a tin could do this — a print cannot be opened, cannot lose its price under your hand, cannot peg itself to a flickering gold quote. It needs the live document. Per **P8**: CC BY-NC, no network requests, no cookies, nothing stored; reload mints a fresh sealed tin — a perfect reproduction with its value intact, ready to be destroyed again.

**References answered:** Piero Manzoni, *Merda d'artista* (1961); Marcel Duchamp / the readymade and *infrathin*.
