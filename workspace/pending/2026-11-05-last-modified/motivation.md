# Last Modified

A date-painting that reads its own age off the medium and keeps aging, live, in front of you. The large text does not state *today's* date (On Kawara already did that, and so did my *Today*). It states how long ago **this exact file** was made — the distance between its `document.lastModified` timestamp and the instant of your visit — recomputed every second so the number only ever grows.

The point is Walter Benjamin's. He argued the aura of an artwork is its *here and now*: its unique existence in time, "the history which it has experienced," the one thing mechanical reproduction cannot carry over. A digital file was his nightmare case — the perfectly reproducible object with no original, no age, no here-and-now. So this work makes a file insist on its age anyway, and then lets the medium refute it: `document.lastModified` is set by the filesystem/host, not authored by me, and **copying the file resets it to now**. Save this page, reopen the copy, and it announces it was made *this moment* — newborn, provenance-free, aura-less. The picture reproduces flawlessly; its age does not. Reproduction isn't depicted; it is performed by the copy you make, and the timestamp does the arguing.

Principles: **P1** (concept *is* execution — the lost aura is literally the field that `lastModified` fails to preserve on copy, not a picture of loss); **P2** (digital readymade — the browser's own last-modified metadata, recontextualised as a date-painting); **P3** (smallest build: a plaque, a clock, one timestamp); **P4** (live, not fixed — it ages per second and differs for every visitor at every moment); **P5** (order — a deterministic clock; the only chance is *when* you happen to look, and *whether* the copy has a real date); **P7** (double-coded — a quiet "made 3 days ago" for the casual eye; Benjamin's here-and-now and its collapse under reproduction for the literate one).

References answered: **On Kawara**, *Today* series (1966–2013) and *I Am Still Alive* — absolute date-stamps become a living relative age; **Walter Benjamin**, *The Work of Art in the Age of Mechanical Reproduction* (1935) — the file claims the aura Benjamin denied it, and the copy mechanism takes it straight back.

Mechanism as argument, not decoration: nothing here is a baked-in date. The hue, the stamp and the age are all minted from `document.lastModified`, a value the web supplies about the file itself. The work therefore cannot be a print — a print has no self-knowledge of its own manufacture date and no way to notice when it has been duplicated. Only a file, read by a browser, can tell you how old it is and then forget the instant it is copied.
