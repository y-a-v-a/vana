# Rotorelief (Scroll to Turn)

In 1935 Duchamp had six cardboard discs printed and sold them, unspectacularly, at a Paris inventors' fair. Flat, they are nothing — off-centre spirals, mild graphic design. Placed on a turntable at 33⅓ rpm, the same ink swells into a beating three-dimensional depth that exists nowhere on the disc and everywhere in the turning. The *Rotoreliefs* are the readymade of motion itself: the artwork is not the object but the rotation the object is subjected to.

This work restages that dependency with the newest thing a browser can do without JavaScript: **CSS scroll-driven animation** (`animation-timeline: scroll(root)`). The disc's spin is bound, degree for degree, to how far the document has been scrolled — and the document is deliberately six screens tall so there is travel for the motor to consume. There is no timer, no `requestAnimationFrame`, no autoplay. The only motor is the reader's scrollbar. Scroll and the spiral swells and recedes; stop and it flattens into exactly what a Rotorelief is off its turntable: a print. This enacts **P1** (concept *is* execution — the scroll mechanism doesn't illustrate rotation, it *is* the rotation), **P2** (a canonical readymade recontextualised through a web-native behaviour), and **P5** on the *order* side (a strict mechanical spiral, driven).

The mechanism is the argument in a second, sharper way. The `@supports (animation-timeline: scroll())` guard means the piece splits by browser: engines that have shipped the spec grant you the turntable; engines that haven't hand you a still drawing and a note saying so — and reduced-motion users are handed the same still drawing, honestly, because a motionless Rotorelief *is* a print and Duchamp would have agreed. The optical depth is never in the pixels; it is manufactured in the eye during the act of looking, on loan from your own scrolling. Nothing is stored, nothing is transmitted, and the JavaScript present does exactly one thing — draw the geometry once — so that the motion can belong entirely to you (**P4**, the work as behaviour rather than artifact; **P8**, open licence, no cookies, no requests).

It answers Duchamp directly, and glances at the whole optical/kinetic lineage (Anémic Cinéma; the later op-kinetic disc). It is distinct from the catalogue's other motion pieces: *Sallie Gardner at a Gallop* quantises scroll into discrete canvas frames in JavaScript to reconstruct Muybridge; here there are no frames and no script-driven motion at all — a continuous analogue spin supplied by a pure-CSS timeline. The cheerful-cynic line is the honest one printed on the wall label: *medium — scroll position on screen; no motor of its own.*
