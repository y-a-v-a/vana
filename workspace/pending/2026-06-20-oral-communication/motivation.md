# Oral Communication

A webpage that talks but will not write, record, store, or transmit a single word of itself. Press the button and the device's own voice says the work aloud — once through, into the room — and then it is gone. There is no transcript on the page (the spoken sentences live only in a JavaScript string, never inserted into the DOM), no audio file, no `MediaRecorder`, no `localStorage`, no network. Reload and it speaks again, but nothing of the previous performance survives. The screen reads only `silence`, then `the work is being spoken`, then `silence — nothing was kept`.

The work answers **Ian Wilson's *Oral Communication* / *The Discussions*** (1968– ), in which spoken language was the sole medium and documentation — photography, recording, even a written record — was refused, leaving an artwork that could not be owned, reproduced, or resold. I drop that gesture onto the most documentational machine ever built. The browser is a perfect copying engine; here it is made to manufacture something it categorically cannot save. That contradiction **is** the argument, not an illustration of it: the mechanism — local `SpeechSynthesis` (the OS voice, offline-preferred via `localService`), deliberately paired with the *absence* of `fetch`, recording, and storage — enacts immateriality on a medium whose whole nature is the copy. **P1** (concept *is* execution): the refusal to persist is wired into the code, not described by it.

It is a **digital readymade** (**P2**): the device's text-to-speech engine, a mundane accessibility primitive, is recontextualised as a fine-art medium that produces unsellable, undocumentable speech. It sits on the chance/order axis (**P5**) at the order pole — a fixed, authored text — but its *output* is partly chance: each device's installed voice, accent, and cadence makes every performance a different recitation the artist never specified. The piece is **minimal** (**P3**): one button, one utterance, an empty stage. It targets value and authenticity (**P6**) by removing the object entirely — the medium label even reads "Edition: none." And it keeps the **web-native ethic** (**P8**): CC BY-NC, no cookies, no trackers, no network, no data; the required user gesture before speaking is itself the house motto, "we do not search, we serve."

It is **double-coded** (**P7**): the casual visitor gets a dry gag — a website that speaks to you but refuses to show or keep its words, *you had to be there* — while the literate viewer reads a precise restaging of conceptual art's immaterial, anti-documentary lineage on the platform least built for it. The voice is the **cheerful cynic** (**P9**): "You cannot save it. You cannot sell it… You were simply present to hear it. That is the whole of the work."

It is materially distinct from its catalogue neighbours. Unlike *Page with the Sound of Its Own Making* (Morris; Web Audio sonifying the page's source as tones), this speaks **language**, not process, via a different API, after a different artist. Unlike *4′33″ (We Do Not Listen)* (Cage; performed silence / no capture), this performs **speech** and orality, not absence of sound. And unlike the text-as-material pieces (*If I Say So*, *Each Being Equal*, *Linea*), the work here is never legible on screen at all — it is only ever heard, and only once.
