# Oil on Viewer

A color-field painting with no fixed appearance. Every colour, every contrast step, and the breathing of the third band are dictated by the viewer's own operating-system settings — `prefers-color-scheme`, `prefers-contrast`, `prefers-reduced-motion`, `inverted-colors`, `forced-colors`, `color-gamut`, `dynamic-range` — read live through `matchMedia` and its `change` events. There are no controls on the page. The only brush is the viewer's system-settings panel. Two people never receive the same painting; one person sees it repaint at dusk if their machine auto-switches to dark mode. Under Windows high-contrast mode the OS seizes the palette outright, and the wall label says so.

This answers **Hans Haacke, *Condensation Cube* (1963–65)** — a sealed system whose image is produced not by the artist's hand but by the ambient conditions of the room. The browser's preference media queries are the web's "ambient conditions," and the live data plate at the foot of the page is a Haacke-style real-time readout of them. It also answers **László Moholy-Nagy, *Telephone Pictures* (1922)**, paintings he ordered from a sign factory by dictating positions on an industrial colour chart, removing his hand from the work. Here the OS accessibility panel is the colour chart and the factory at once.

The mechanism *is* the argument (P1): the painting is not decorated by the settings, it is literally composed of them — the media-query truth values are both the pigment and the wall text. This stages the chance/order axis (P5): the artist supplies strict, deterministic composition rules (an order), and the viewer's contingent environment supplies the values that fill them (the chance) — yet identical settings always yield the identical canvas, so it is a reproducible *portrait of a configuration*, not noise. It is a digital readymade of the viewer's own machine state (P2), and the smallest build that completes the thought: no images, no libraries, no randomness (P3).

The 2026 turn is ethical and is also content (P8). `prefers-color-scheme`, `prefers-reduced-motion`, gamut and contrast are exactly the entropy that fingerprinting scripts harvest to identify you. This work reads every one of them to your face, paints with them, and sends none of them anywhere — no cookies, no storage, no network request of any kind. The signals usually taken silently are here displayed and refused. Double-coded throughout (P7): the casual viewer gets a quiet, responsive color-field that answers their dark mode; the literate viewer gets institutional critique, the removal of the artist's hand, and a small dry joke in the medium line — *Oil on Viewer. Dimensions variable. Edition: one, yours, for as long as you keep them.*
