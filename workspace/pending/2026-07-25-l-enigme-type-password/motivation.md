# L'Énigme (type=password)

In 1920 Man Ray photographed a sewing machine wrapped in an army blanket and bound
with rope — *L'Énigme d'Isidore Ducasse* — and never untied it. The title cites
Lautréamont's line about beauty as "the chance meeting, on a dissecting-table, of a
sewing machine and an umbrella." The whole charge of the object is that its contents
are withheld: you see a shape, you infer, you never confirm. The rope is the artwork.

This work takes that exact gesture and hands the rope to the browser. The concealed
text is Lautréamont's sentence, sitting whole and legible in the document source
inside a single `<input type="password">`. Nothing hides it but the platform: the
browser renders each character as a bullet, so you get the bundle's *length* — its
silhouette — but not its meaning. A "cut the rope" button flips the input to
`type="text"` and the enigma evaporates in one click. Man Ray's mystery was
permanent; the digital wrap is a costume that undoes itself.

The mechanism **is** the argument (P1, P2 — a digital readymade of the password
field): concealment is not drawn by CSS decoration but performed by the browser's own
masking, the same routine that hides your credentials. The page never conceals
anything — it publishes the sentence in plain source — so the withholding is the
medium's, not the maker's, which is the point. It sits on the chance/order axis (P5)
by way of its own text: the sentence *about* the chance encounter is itself locked
behind an ordered, deterministic mask. And it reads at two depths (P7): the casual
viewer meets a wrapped bundle of dots that unwraps to a French sentence; the literate
viewer meets Man Ray, Lautréamont, and Benjamin's aura dissolving the instant a work
becomes trivially reproducible — here, one click of reveal.

Smallest build that completes the thought (P3): one text input, one toggle, one live
count of the withheld glyphs. Open licence, no cookies, no network, no data off the
device (P8). It answers Man Ray's sealed object with the web's un-sealable one.
