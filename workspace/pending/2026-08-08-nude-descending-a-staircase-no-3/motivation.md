# Nude Descending a Staircase (No. 3)

In 1912 Duchamp did the impossible for paint: he recorded motion. *Nude Descending a Staircase, No. 2* superimposes roughly twenty frozen phases of a body coming down a flight of stairs into one permanent, simultaneous picture — the descent held forever, all at once. This piece inverts that gesture with the one web mechanism built to do exactly the opposite. The [View Transition API](https://developer.mozilla.org/docs/Web/API/View_Transition_API) snapshots the figure at the top and at the bottom of the tread, and the browser *invents* every frame in between. The document itself only ever contains two poses. The actual "nude descending" — the interstitial phases Duchamp labored to make eternal — is composited by your machine and destroyed the instant it arrives.

The mechanism **is** the argument (**P1**). I did not draw the descent; the compositor did, off-thread, and kept nothing. A live readout reports how many frames your browser rendered for each descent and then discarded — a number the platform will not otherwise disclose — beside a permanent "kept: 0." Nothing is stored, cached, or sent (**P8**): the descent has no persistence by design, so the work is a behaviour and not an artifact (**P4**, "only change"). This sits squarely on the chance↔order axis (**P5**): the staircase treads are a strict rule-based ladder (order), while the exact frames the browser interpolates are non-deterministic, device-dependent, and unrepeatable (chance).

It is a digital readymade (**P2**): I appropriate a named canonical painting and a stock browser animation primitive and let their opposite intentions collide. The double coding (**P7**) is that a casual viewer sees a little cubist figure walking down some stairs; a literate viewer sees that the museum's most famous *record* of motion has been re-staged by the one machine that refuses to record. Under `prefers-reduced-motion`, the browser declines to draw the descent at all and shows only the endpoints — accessibility as material, and the dry punchline (**P9**) that some viewers are shown, faithfully, no descent whatsoever.

**Reference answered:** Marcel Duchamp, *Nude Descending a Staircase, No. 2* (1912) — the cheeky "No. 3" continues his own sequel-numbering. Duchamp is the project's conceptual root; this rhymes his frozen-motion painting against the web's throwaway-motion engine, and against y-a-v-a's own *Images That Never Were* (the frames here, too, never were).

**Distinct from the catalogue:** it is not scroll-quantized frames you drive (*Sallie Gardner at a Gallop*), not one transition causing the next (*Der Lauf der Dinge / transitionend*), and not a flicker cycle (*The webpage going on and off*). The subject is specifically the browser-invented, immediately-discarded *interpolated* frames of a named motion-painting.
