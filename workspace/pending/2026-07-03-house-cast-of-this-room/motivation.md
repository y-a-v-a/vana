# House (Cast of This Room)

Rachel Whiteread makes sculpture out of the space we are trained to ignore: *Ghost* (1990) is a plaster cast of the inside of a room, and *House* (1993) was a concrete cast of the entire interior of a Victorian terrace — after which the terrace itself was demolished and only the negative stood, alone, in the street. The subject is the air, the void, the between; the price of it is the original. This work re-pours that gesture in the browser. A page of text is a room. Its true material is not the words but the emptiness around them — the margins, the leading, the gaps a reader steps over. Press the button and that emptiness is filled with concrete; the room is then torn down, and what remains is the cast: the air made solid, the text made hole.

The mechanism *is* the argument (**P1**). `getBoundingClientRect()` is the caliper: the browser reports where each block actually landed in *this* layout, and every measured box is cleared out of a poured concrete field, so the positive content survives only as the shape of its own absence. The measurement is taken live and per visitor, so the cast is native to wherever the page was laid out — no two demolitions match, and it could never be a print (**G1**, **P4**). Then the demolition is literal: the living DOM room is removed, and the cast is left as a frozen canvas bitmap. From that moment the concrete is rigid while the viewport is not — resize the window and the slab refuses to reflow, exposing it as a solid object photographed against the dark, not a responsive page. That divergence between a frozen raster and a live layout is a specifically web-native way to say *this is the mould, not the room*.

It answers Whiteread directly, and behind her Duchamp's readymade (the DOM is the found object) and Benjamin's anxiety about the original destroyed to make the copy (**P2**, narrative): here the copy is the only thing that was ever made, and the original is deleted to produce it. It sits on the order↔chance axis (**P5**) — a strict measuring rule on one side, aggregate grain and a per-visit resin tint on the other — and it keeps the restraint the DNA prizes (**P3**): one button, one pour, one demolition, no persistence.

The cheerful cynic is in the terms of trade (**P9**). Whiteread's *House* was demolished by the council within months; the label here promises the same fate and then offers the internet's cheapest miracle — reload and the destroyed thing comes back, perfect, free, forever.
