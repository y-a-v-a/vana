# Cerca Trova

The page shows a wall. On it, Vasari's green flag: **CERCA TROVA** — *seek, and you shall find*. Nothing else appears to be there. But Leonardo's *Battle of Anghiari* **is** on this page: it lives inside a region marked `hidden="until-found"`, present in the DOM, taking no space and never painted, sealed like a mural behind a fresco. The only tool that can reach it is the browser's own Find-in-page. When you press Ctrl/Cmd-F and search for the word on the flag, the browser fires `beforematch`, strips the hidden state, and the buried cavalry surfaces through the wall. Reload and it sinks back.

This enacts **P1 — concept *is* execution**: the found-content mechanism is not a metaphor for the lost painting, it *is* the sealed layer, and the act of searching *is* Vasari's *cerca trova*. It is a **P2 digital readymade** — the browser's find feature and a famous lost masterpiece, recontextualised — and a **P4** behaviour rather than an artifact: the work only occurs in the moment of being sought, and cannot be photographed into existence. It is **P3**-minimal: one hidden attribute and one event carry the whole thought.

The reference it answers is precise: **Leonardo da Vinci's *The Battle of Anghiari* (1505, lost)**, overpainted by **Giorgio Vasari's *Battle of Marciano* (1563)**, whose minuscule painted banner reading *cerca trova* has been argued for centuries — most recently by Maurizio Seracini — to mark the Leonardo surviving behind the wall. The web's Find function is that argument made literal: seek and you shall find, on faith, without ever seeing the wall opened.

It double-codes cleanly (**P7**): a casual visitor gets the gag — *you have to search the page to find the painting* — while the literate visitor gets the Anghiari/Vasari/Seracini argument about buried masterpieces and the value we grant to the merely rumoured (a glance at **P6**). The tone stays cheerful-cynic: on browsers that lack `beforematch` (historically Firefox and Safari), the hidden layer can never be found, so the page simply reports that the Leonardo stays lost — a limitation adopted honestly as the concept rather than papered over.
