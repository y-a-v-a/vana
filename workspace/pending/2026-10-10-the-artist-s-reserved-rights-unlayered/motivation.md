# The Artist's Reserved Rights (Unlayered)

A single coloured square — the work — whose appearance is contested by the CSS cascade itself. The artist names it as precisely as the language permits (`figure.frame .work#piece`, specificity 1,1,1) and paints it red. The market names it only `.work` (specificity 0,1,0) and paints it the grey of having been sold. By every measure of specificity the artist should win; the square is grey anyway, because the market's rule lives in a layer declared *after* the artist's, and in CSS Cascade Level 5 a later `@layer` beats a more specific rule in an earlier one. Ownership, the page notes, is not specificity — it is order of declaration. One checkbox, "Invoke the Artist's Reserved Rights, 1971," activates a rule written *outside every layer*; because unlayered declarations outrank all layered ones, it is the only thing in the whole document that can overrule the market and return the square to red.

This enacts **P1** (concept *is* execution: the cascade's precedence rules literally decide who controls the work, so the mechanism is the argument, not an illustration of it) and **P2** (the digital readymade — the CSS cascade, ordinary browser plumbing, appropriated whole as the material). It is the smallest build that completes the thought (**P3**): one square, two layered rules, one unlayered escape hatch, zero JavaScript. It sits squarely on the **order** pole of the chance/order axis (**P5**) — a cascade is a strict, deterministic ordering system, identical in every conforming engine — and it aims at **P6**, value and the power to control a work after it leaves the artist's hands. It is double-coded (**P7**): a casual viewer gets a SOLD square that a box marked "reserved rights" un-sells, while a CSS-literate viewer reads a precise lesson in layer precedence, and both land without a separate wall text.

It answers a named reference in each of its two registers. In art-world history it answers the **Siegelaub–Projansky *Artist's Reserved Rights Transfer and Sale Agreement* (1971)** — the contract conceptual-art dealer Seth Siegelaub commissioned to give artists continuing rights and control *outside* the ordinary transfer of ownership. The agreement maps onto the mechanism exactly: the normal cascade (artist overruled by the later-declared market) is the ordinary sale; the unlayered rule is the special instrument that alone stands above it. The page keeps the agreement's real-world footnote — "almost no one ever did" — as its dry, cheerful-cynic close (**P9**). In the net.art register it continues the "browser plumbing as material" line (Socle du Monde's `<base>`, Vir Heroicus Sublimis's scrollbar) and the twenty-year market thread (Art is Money, Hammer Price, Merda d'artista), while using a mechanism none of them touch.

The argument could not survive as a print. A static red or grey square on paper says nothing; the work exists only because view-source shows the artist's objectively-stronger rule being silently overpowered by declaration order, and because a live cascade recomputes who wins the instant you file the 1971 contract. The medium — the order in which style is declared — is the whole of the claim about who owns the look of a thing after it is sold.
