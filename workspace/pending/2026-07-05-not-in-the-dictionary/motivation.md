# Not in the Dictionary

A short manifesto lists the words art invented — *Merz*, *Zaum*, *Rayonism*, *Neoplasticism*, *Tachisme*, *Merzbau*, *infrathin*, and the artist's own coinages *Maleglitch* and *Modernipsum*. The text sits in a live `contenteditable` element with `spellcheck="true"`. The browser, consulting a dictionary it was born with, disagrees, and draws a red line under each word it does not recognise. The only red on the page is the machine's: a colour I never chose, applied to a canon I did.

**The mechanism is the argument (P1, G3).** This is not a picture of correction; it *is* correction, performed live by the viewer's own operating system. The point turns on an infrathin fact of the platform: those red squiggles are painted by the OS text layer, *outside the DOM*. No node, no attribute, no computed style records them, and there is no API to ask. So the page can show a manifesto and cannot read its own condemnation. The "Show me which words you flagged" button proves it — a real DOM search that returns zero. The correction is applied to you and the record is kept from you; the dictionary that decides what counts as a word is one you did not pick and cannot open. That un-accountability, not the underline, is the work.

**Digital readymade (P2), minimal build (P3), double-coded (P7).** The readymade here is the browser's spellchecker — the most ubiquitous, least-examined act of gatekeeping on the web, promoted to institution. The casual viewer gets a clean gag: art's grandest words marked "wrong," including *y-a-v-a* itself. The literate viewer gets the argument that lexicography is enclosure — every avant-garde word begins as an error the dictionary refuses to admit. The build is the smallest that completes the thought: one editable element, one attribute, one honest button.

**Reference it answers.** The headline is Kurt Schwitters' *Merz* (1919), a meaningless fragment sliced out of "Commerzbank" and declared a movement — a word every dictionary still underlines. Around it stand the manifesto neologisms of Dada and Zaum and Duchamp's *infrathin*. And it answers a prior y-a-v-a work, *Modernipsum* (2012), which generated art-ism language; this piece hands that same language to the machine and lets the machine judge it. It sits on the order↔chance axis (P5) on the side of order — a fixed, invisible rule set deciding what is a word — and points, per P6/P8, at authority and its accountability: the license is open (CC BY-SA), and nothing you type is stored, read, or sent.
