# Untitled (Placeholder)

The whole work is a single HTML `placeholder` attribute. By specification, placeholder text is shown *only when the control has no value* — it is a hint, never content. So the artwork exists exclusively in the moment before you contribute anything. The instant you type one character, your value takes the field and the work is gone; empty the field and it returns intact. The text is also, by the same spec, unselectable and uncopyable, and it belongs to no author node in the DOM — it is an attribute, not a paragraph. Present only in absence, uncopyable, unauthored: the mechanism *is* the argument (P1).

This answers relational aesthetics head-on. Rirkrit Tiravanija's *Untitled (Free)* (1992) and Nicolas Bourriaud's *Relational Aesthetics* (1998) hold that the artwork is constituted by the viewer's participation — the meal cooked, the exchange had. Here participation does the opposite: it annihilates the piece. The empty invitation is the art; filling it in is vandalism. It is a cheerful-cynic rebuttal to the entire user-generated-content and "we do not search, we serve" web, and, obliquely, to the blank AI prompt box we are all forever asked to fill (P9, P6). The interface itself — the empty form field — is the digital readymade (P2).

The double coding is immediate (P7): a casual viewer sees a big grey message, goes to type, and watches their own keystrokes erase it — the joke lands without a wall text. A literate viewer reads the argument about authorship (the text has no author element), reproducibility (you cannot copy it — Benjamin's aura restored by a browser quirk), and the myth of the participatory artwork. A live status line reports how much of the work you have "replaced," and the piece heals the moment you empty the field — behaviour, not artifact (P4). It sits toward the *order* pole of the chance↔order axis, its only chance being a per-visit accent hue.

Smallest build that completes the thought (P3): one textarea, one CSS pseudo-element, a dozen lines of script. No storage, no cookies, no network, no persistence — reload and the work is intact again, as if you were never here (P8).
