# Each Being Equal

A digital readymade of Lawrence Weiner's *Declaration of Intent* (1968) — the founding clause of language-as-sculpture: *"1. The artist may construct the piece. 2. The piece may be fabricated. 3. The piece need not be built. Each being equal and consistent with the intent of the artist, the decision as to condition rests with the receiver upon the occasion of receipt."* Weiner gave the receiver the power to decide whether the work is ever materialised. The web is the medium that finally takes him at his word: every page is text received by a stranger, and every button promises that a click will *do something*.

This work mints a fresh Weiner-style statement per visit from a small material/action/surface grammar (e.g. `LAMP BLACK SCATTERED ACROSS A WALL`) and declares the work **complete the instant you read it** — receipt, not construction, completes it. It then offers Weiner's three conditions as live buttons and hands you the decision. Whichever you press, the statement never renders as an object and the receipt reads `THE WORK IS UNCHANGED`. The "construct" button constructs nothing — not from a defect but because the theory insists all three conditions are *equal*.

**Principles enacted.** P1 (concept *is* execution): the web's core affordance — interactivity that resolves to nothing — is the literal content of "each being equal"; the dead button *is* the argument, not a decoration on it. P2 (digital readymade): a named conceptual work recontextualised through platform logic. P3 (minimal gesture): one page, a grammar, three buttons, no build step completes the thought. P5 (order): a strict rule-based language system in the Weiner / systems-art lineage. P9 (humour as carrier): the cheerful-cynic gag — a call-to-action that, by design and by theory, refuses to act — carries a real claim about conceptual art and about the web's manufactured sense of agency.

**The mechanism is the message.** A static print of a Weiner statement is just a Weiner statement. Here the page *withholds the consequence of the click* and reports its own indifference, which is something only an interactive, served, per-visit medium can perform. It answers Weiner directly (and nods to net.art's treatment of platform behaviour as material): the decision genuinely rests with the receiver, and the receiver discovers their decision was always immaterial.

**Reference answered:** Lawrence Weiner, *Declaration of Intent* (1968); language-as-material / Conceptual art; net.art (UI affordance as medium).
