# Inert Gas (From a Measured Volume to Indefinite Expansion)

In 1969 **Robert Barry** drove into the Mojave Desert and released measured volumes of the noble gases — helium, neon, argon, krypton, xenon — into the open air. The *Inert Gas Series* existed completely and disappeared instantly: the gas expanded without limit, invisible, unownable, beyond retrieval. The artwork was real, and it was gone, and both at once. This piece restages that gesture (**P2**, digital readymade) inside the browser.

The mechanism is the argument (**P1**). The whole work runs on a single web-native primitive: the HTML **`inert`** attribute. An inert element is not deleted — it stays in the DOM, it stays visible — but it is removed from the focus order, it ignores pointer events, its text can no longer be selected, and crucially it leaves the **accessibility tree**, the layer where screen readers and other perceiving instruments find a page at all. *Present and unreachable* is not a metaphor I bolted on; it is the literal, specified behaviour of `inert`. Releasing a canister sets it inert and lets it drift and blur away like rising gas. You can confirm the dematerialization yourself: before release you can Tab to a gas and "touch" it; after release the same element answers nothing, because it is no longer in the page's reachable world. The reachable/inert tally is computed by reading the real `.inert` property, so the readout cannot lie. At the end the apparatus — wall label, button, counter — goes inert too: the work becomes everything you can see and nothing you can touch, and only reload, an act *outside* the document, can refill the canisters.

It answers Barry directly and sits squarely in the dematerialization-of-the-art-object lineage the catalogue already tracks (Ian Wilson's *Oral Communication*, Manzoni's *Linea*, Barry's own *During the exhibition the gallery will be closed*). On the chance/order axis (**P5**) it is pure order: a measured, deterministic release that produces indefinite expansion — Barry's own subtitle, kept verbatim. It is materially distinct from the catalogue's other Barry piece, which runs on `navigator.onLine`; this one runs on `inert`, a different mechanism enacting a different work.

The double coding (**P7**) is the engine. On the surface it is a dry pun any developer gets at a glance — *inert gas* released by the `inert` attribute (**P9**). Underneath it asks where an artwork actually *is* once it can be seen but not held, indexed but not reached: the accessibility tree turns out to be a real exhibition space, and to release something into the atmosphere is to evict it from there. The build is the smallest that completes the thought (**P3**): one attribute, no canvas, no storage, no network, CC BY-SA, no cookies, attribution to Barry and y-a-v-a stated on the page (**P8**).
