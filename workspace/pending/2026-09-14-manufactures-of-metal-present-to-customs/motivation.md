# Manufactures of Metal (Present to Customs)

In 1926 Edward Steichen shipped Constantin Brâncuși's *Bird in Space* into New York. U.S. Customs refused to enter it as a duty-free work of art. To their appraiser it was a polished lump of bronze — a *manufacture of metal* under §399 of the Tariff Act, taxable at 40% ad valorem — and it was reportedly logged under "Kitchen Utensils and Hospital Supplies." *Brancusi v. United States* (1928) settled the question in court: the object was admitted, free of duty, as art. The bronze never changed. Only the ruling did.

This work makes the browser the customs official. The mechanism is the **Constraint Validation API**: whatever you type into the declaration field, the submit handler calls `setCustomValidity()` with the customs reclassification and `reportValidity()` raises the browser's **native validation bubble** — an OS/vendor-drawn popup you cannot restyle, reword, or refuse. The verdict on what your object *is* is delivered in an authority's voice, not yours; leave the field empty and the browser answers in its own factory line ("Please fill out this field"). The gatekeeping mechanism *is* the argument (**P1**): art-status here is not a property of the thing but a classification imposed from outside, exactly as it was on the pier. This is a digital readymade of the border checkpoint (**P2**), the smallest build that completes the thought (**P3**), and it targets value and authenticity directly (**P6**) — a fixed $240 duty, art vs. commodity, decided by a clerk.

It is double-coded (**P7**): a casual viewer gets the deadpan joke of a masterpiece booked as a kitchen utensil; the literate viewer knows Brâncuși *won*, and finds the "Appeal to the Court" button, which sets `setCustomValidity('')` — the field is valid, the object admitted. The historical resolution is enacted, not narrated: the same object, unaltered, crosses the line the instant the law relents.

It answers **Marcel Duchamp / the readymade** (an object becomes art by declaration and context) and **Walter Benjamin** (the appraiser's charge — it "looks manufactured" — is the reproducibility anxiety in a tariff schedule), and it sits beside y-a-v-a's own value pieces (*Art is Money*, *Hammer Price*) and its "but is it art" line (*But is it art?*, *Tertium*) while using a mechanism none of them touch. It is web-native and could not be a print: the ruling is issued live by the reader's own browser engine, in the reader's own OS chrome.
