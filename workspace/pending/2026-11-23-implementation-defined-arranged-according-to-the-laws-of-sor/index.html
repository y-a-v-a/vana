Implementation-Defined (Arranged According to the Laws of Sort)

y-a-v-a, 2026

Twelve squares thrown into twelve places, again and again, by your browser. Each cell: how often square row landed in place column. Grey is fair. Black is too often. Paper is too seldom.

Left, honest chance: the more it throws, the less there is to see. Right, the shortcut everyone copies: a sorting algorithm fed a coin. The specification calls the result “implementation-defined”. Your engine defines it here. True chance paints nothing; the bug paints a portrait of the sorter.

Medium: Array.prototype.sort, Math.random, your JavaScript engine, ongoing. Edition: one per engine.

After Hans (Jean) Arp, Untitled (Collage with Squares Arranged According to the Laws of Chance), 1916–17; and the 2010 EU Browser Choice ballot, whose “random” order was shuffled the same way.
