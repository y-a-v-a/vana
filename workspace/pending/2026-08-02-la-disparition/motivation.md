# La Disparition

A writing surface that silently refuses one letter. Type freely — but the fifth glyph of the Latin alphabet, the most common letter in English and in French, never arrives. Each `beforeinput` event is intercepted before the character can land; the letter flashes up as a grey ghost at the caret, drifts, and vanishes. A quiet counter tallies how many have been cast out. The page's own instructions are themselves a lipogram, written without the letter, as proof the constraint is real and self-applied.

The work answers Georges Perec's *La Disparition* (1969) — a 300-page novel composed entirely without the letter E — and the OuLiPo's practice of constraint-as-generator (**P5**, hard toward the *order* pole: a strict rule that produces the writing rather than decorates it). In Perec the missing letter is never only formal: E stands for *eux*, "them", the family the war disappeared. The work stays dry rather than maudlin (**P9**), letting the single restrained line in the wall label carry the weight while the surface delivers the gag: "the most common letter. Good luck."

The mechanism *is* the argument (**P1, P3**). Perec's constraint was a private discipline he had to keep by hand; here the browser keeps it for you, absolutely, by editing the input stream before it reaches the document. You do not read *about* absence — you hit a wall where a letter should be, and watch it disappear at the caret. That refusal is a thing only a live medium can do (**G1**): a print cannot swallow your keystroke, cannot show you the ghost of what it took. It is the smallest build that completes the thought — one event handler enacts a whole poetics.

The colophon closes the double-coding (**P7**). The label must name Perec, Vincent, Creative Commons — words that cannot avoid the forbidden letter — so every E it contains is greyed out in place: the letters the poem above could not hold, returned as a memorial in the apparatus. The frame mourns what the picture had to exclude. It answers the same lineage as *Modernipsum* (2012, language toy) and *Book from the Sky* (glyph absence) but by a distinct mechanism — selective, live keystroke refusal — and a reference the catalogue has never named (**G6**).
