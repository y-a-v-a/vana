# Touch Piece (Do Not Look)

An artwork with nothing to see. Press the button while holding your phone and the work is performed once — as a pulse in the device's vibration motor, a private tremor in your hand. It has no image, no sound, no file. It is generated fresh for this visit from cryptographic entropy, held only in a closure, drawn nowhere, stored nowhere, sent nowhere. When the buzzing stops, the work is over. Reload and a different, unrepeatable one is minted; leave the tab and it dies.

The mechanism is the argument (**P1**). The entire work is `navigator.vibrate` — an API that only exists on a phone held in a hand, that browsers gate behind a user gesture, and that records nothing. So the medium itself decides who the work exists for. On a desktop, in Safari, on an iPhone, `'vibrate' in navigator` is false and the page tells you the plain truth: *for you, this work does not exist* — there is nothing hidden on the page to uncover. The dematerialization is not depicted; it is enforced by the platform. This is the **digital readymade** (**P2**) of the phone's buzzer, and the **smallest build that completes the thought** (**P3**): no canvas, no picture of a pulse, just the pulse.

It answers **Yoko Ono's *Grapefruit* instruction pieces** (1964) — imperatives to be completed in the body and the mind rather than looked at ("Touch Piece," "Painting to Be Stepped On") — and **Lucy Lippard's *Six Years: The Dematerialization of the Art Object*** (1966–72). Ono asked for the artwork to happen off the wall; the browser here obliges literally, moving the whole work into a haptic motor that the eye can never reach. The per-visit random score puts the piece on the **chance** end of the chance/order axis (**P5**): the composition is irreproducible by construction, gone the instant the motor rests.

And it is dry rather than sentimental (**P9**). A pulse in the palm invites a heartbeat reading; the piece refuses it — the rhythm is meaningless noise from `crypto.getRandomValues`, attested on screen only by three blinking dots and the flat line *"It is over. Nothing was kept."* The market question sits underneath (**P6/P7**): here is an artwork that cannot be photographed, downloaded, screenshotted, hung, or resold — its only site of exhibition is one motor, one hand, one moment. There is nothing to authenticate and nothing to own.
