# I Do Not Wish to Add Any More

In 1969 Douglas Huebler wrote the line that emptied a generation of studios: *"The world is full of objects, more or less interesting; I do not wish to add any more."* Conceptual art's great refusal — to stop manufacturing things. The web is the exact opposite machine. A page is an accretion engine; the DOM is a structure that can only grow; `appendChild` is the gesture every site performs all day. This work makes Huebler's sentence the physical law of a single document.

A `MutationObserver` watches the page and removes any element added to it — anything without the `data-original` mark applied to the world as it already stood at load. The button labelled *Add an object to the world* really does append a small black square; the observer really does state it out of existence, one frame later. The "Objects in the world" tally never moves. "Objects refused" climbs. The refusals accumulate as a number — Huebler's preferred medium, *"the existence of things in terms of time and/or place"* — while no object is ever added. Open the console and `document.body.appendChild(...)` yourself: same verdict. The page cannot be made fuller.

This enacts **P1** (concept *is* execution — the observer is not illustrating the refusal, it *is* the refusal) and **P3** (smallest gesture: a seal and a tally). It sits at the **order** pole of **P5** — a single inviolable rule, applied without exception — and it answers **P2/P10**'s question about value (**P6**) from the supply side: where the market measures art by what is added, here the only growth permitted is the count of things turned away. The reference it answers is named on the page: Douglas Huebler's January 1969 statement for Seth Siegelaub, with a glance at Lawrence Weiner's *Declaration of Intent* in the spirit of a rule that "need not be built."

The mechanism is the argument, not decoration: every other catalogued removal piece destroys what is present (*Acid Action Painting*, *Inert Gas*); this one is homeostatic — it refuses accretion, the web's one native verb. **P7** double-coding: the casual viewer presses a button that does nothing and shrugs; the literate viewer recognises that the nothing is the whole of Conceptualism's exit from object-making, automated as browser physics. **P9** cheerful cynic: a gallery that proudly counts what it will not let you give it. Open licence, no storage, no network — the world stays exactly as full as it already was.
