# Esse Est Percipi (Rendered on Sight)

**Concept.** A gallery hangs one hundred paintings, and at any moment almost all of them do not exist. The page uses the CSS property `content-visibility: auto`, under which the browser is spec-permitted to skip layout and paint for any panel outside (or near-outside) the viewport. What you are not looking at is not rendered. Each canvas is minted from chance — a per-visit `crypto.getRandomValues` composition, ground plus a few thrown rectangles — the instant it enters your view, and is un-drawn and forgotten the instant it leaves. Scroll back and a *different* painting is born in the same slot; no two glances land on the same picture twice. A fixed census reports the live population: "perceived — therefore rendered: N / 100."

**The mechanism is the argument (P1, P2).** This is not a picture *of* Berkeley's idealism; it is idealism executed by the rendering engine. Bishop Berkeley held *esse est percipi* — to be is to be perceived — and the joke the work lands is that the browser vendors have quietly agreed with him, for reasons of battery and frame budget. `content-visibility` is a performance optimisation (P2, the digital readymade): appropriated wholesale, its literal operation — "do not render the unperceived" — is a metaphysics. My JavaScript does not draw the unseen; it cannot, because the engine has already refused. I merely take the census and, on `contentvisibilityautostatechange`, mint or cease. Where the browser is a "naive realist" (no support), the page says so and paints all hundred — the anti-pattern becomes the second half of the gag (P9).

**Principles enacted.** P1 (concept *is* execution: the render loop withholds being); P2 (a spec'd browser optimisation as readymade); P3 (the smallest build — one CSS property does the ontology, JS only witnesses); P4 (live, not fixed — paintings exist only while looked at, "only change"); P5 (chance/order: the ordered numbered grid of one hundred slots against a chance-thrown composition minted fresh each perception, held only in RAM, physically irreproducible); P7 (double-coding: a casual viewer sees a corner counter that never reaches 100 and gets the gag; a literate viewer gets Berkeley, and the Ronald Knox "tree in the quad" limerick printed on the wall as the exact rebuttal-and-reply).

**References it answers.** George Berkeley, *A Treatise Concerning the Principles of Human Knowledge* (1710), and the "tree in the quad" limerick attributed to Ronald Knox. In the net-native lineage it deliberately answers y-a-v-a's own *The Creative Act* (2026): that work used a JavaScript `IntersectionObserver` to paint *proportionally* to how much of a single frame the spectator sees, after Duchamp and Eco's open work — authorship shared with the beholder. This work does the opposite and simpler thing: it hands the metaphysics to the browser's *own* CSS containment behaviour, not my observer, and makes existence **binary and vendor-enforced** — a hundred works blinking in and out of being as a side effect of a scrollbar and a power-saving heuristic. The cheerful-cynic reading: your rendering engine is a subjective idealist, and it is one to save power.

**Ethics.** CC BY-SA 4.0, stated on the page. No cookies, no storage, no trackers, no network requests of any kind; the paintings live only in RAM and only while perceived.
