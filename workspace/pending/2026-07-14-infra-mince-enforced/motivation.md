# Infra-mince (Enforced)

A tight loop reads the browser's high-resolution clock (`performance.now()`) as fast as it can and records the interval it reports between consecutive reads. Most of the time that interval is **zero** — the loop did real work, but the browser insists no time passed. Then the clock lurches forward by a fixed quantum, and lurches again by multiples of it. Plotted live, the intervals fall on a comb: 0, one quantum, two quanta, three. The width of one tooth is exhibited, in microseconds, as the artwork. It is different on your machine than on the next.

The concept **is** the execution (**P1**). Duchamp's *inframince* — his term for the smallest conceivable difference, the warmth left in a just-vacated chair, the two faces of a sheet of paper — is here not evoked but *measured and enforced*. Since the Spectre / Meltdown disclosures of 2018, browsers deliberately coarsen and jitter their timers so a page cannot use timing to fingerprint the viewer or read secrets across the machine. That defensive blur puts a hard floor under measurable time: below one quantum, two genuinely distinct instants are declared the same instant. The browser has quantified the infra-mince and set its price in microseconds — and it did so, precisely, to stop the medium being used against you (**P8**). The page merely walks up to that floor and hits it; you watch it fail to go finer. There is no decoration between the idea and the mechanism.

It answers **Marcel Duchamp** (the *inframince* notes, c. 1935) and its web-native reference is the **timer-resolution reduction shipped by browser vendors after Spectre / Meltdown (2018)**; it also extends **Mel Bochner's** *Measurement* series (1968–69) from space into time — a dimension line drawn on the instant instead of the room. It sits cleanly on the chance↔order axis (**P5**): the quantisation is pure order (a fixed grid), while the *jitter* browsers add for defence is pure chance (entropy injected to defeat the attacker), and the comb you see is the two contending in one histogram.

Double-coded (**P7**): a casual viewer reads "your browser thinks the smallest moment is 100 µs, and here it is, ticking"; a literate viewer gets Duchamp's infra-mince made computationally literal and the surveillance-mitigation politics underneath it. It is the smallest build that completes the thought (**P3**) — one number, one comb, one label — and it is live, per-visit, and stored nowhere (**P4**): no network, no cookies, no storage, because "measure the viewer precisely" is exactly the act the medium has been rebuilt to refuse.
