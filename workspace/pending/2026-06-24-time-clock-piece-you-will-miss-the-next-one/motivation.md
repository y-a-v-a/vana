# Time Clock Piece (You Will Miss the Next One)

From 11 April 1980 to 11 April 1981, Tehching Hsieh punched a worker's time clock every hour, on the hour, for a full year — 8,760 punches, each one a single film frame, sleep his only enemy. He landed 8,627. The work's authority comes precisely from the 133 he missed: the gaps prove a real, exhausted body was keeping the impossible appointment. This piece hands the visitor the same clock and the same impossibility, and lets the browser do the bookkeeping Hsieh did with his own life.

The mechanism *is* the argument (**P1**). A punch button is enabled only during the first sixty seconds of each clock hour; the rest of the time the window is simply closed and the button is dead. `localStorage` anchors your "performance" to the top of the hour you first arrived, and on every load it computes how many hours have elapsed since — marking each of those hours, retroactively and irrevocably, as **MISSED** unless you were present to punch it. You do not have to do anything to fail; the system clock manufactures absence on its own. The year-grid of 8,760 cells fills with red the instant time passes, and there is no make-up hour. This is endurance art's real subject — discipline measured as the accumulation of what you could not keep — rendered as a machine that scores you as a failure by default (**P5**, the order pole: a rigid, unforgiving grid; **P6**, authenticity that cannot be faked, bought, or back-dated; **P3**, one button, one clock, one grid).

It answers Tehching Hsieh's *One Year Performance 1980–1981 (Time Clock Piece)* directly, and stands beside On Kawara's date discipline as its harsher cousin: Kawara only had to make one painting before midnight; Hsieh had to wake every hour. The web-native twist (**P4**, **P10**-adjacent in spirit) is that the relentless server of time here is `Date.now()` itself — no network, no documentation reel, no witness. The honesty Hsieh secured with photographs is here unprovable and unsharable: your near-empty grid lives in one browser and evaporates when the cache clears, which is the cheerful-cynic punchline. The piece offers you the most rigorous artwork you will never complete, and a clock that has already started without asking.

Double-coded (**P7**): the casual viewer meets a punch clock that visibly refuses them most of the time and a grid bleeding red — an immediate, funny humiliation. The literate viewer reads a precise restaging of durational performance and its core paradox — that the value of such work lies in the misses, and that no copy of the discipline can be transmitted, only the count of its failures.
