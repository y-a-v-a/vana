# Verb List (Actions to Relate to a Page)

In 1967–68 Richard Serra wrote a column of transitive verbs — *to roll, to crease, to fold, to twist, to shorten, to scatter, to remove* — and treated the list itself as sculpture. The verbs were not descriptions of finished objects; they were actions to perform on raw material, and the resulting Anti-Form heaps were just the residue of having done them. The process was the work.

This piece takes Serra's score at his word and notices that the web browser is already a verb list. *To rotate*, *to skew*, *to shorten*, *to smear*, *to scatter*, *to remove* are not metaphors here — they are the literal vocabulary of the rendering engine, the exact operations it performs on real material every frame. So the verbs are not printed and explained; they are wired to the thing they name. Click *to twist* and a CSS skew genuinely twists the slab; click *to remove* and a real node leaves the DOM and does not come back until reload. The score executes on its own body. This is why it could never be a print: a print can depict a twist, but only the browser can actually *do* "to twist" to live matter, on demand, accumulating.

The mechanism **is** the argument (P1, P2 — the engine's transform stack as digital readymade). Each click compounds onto the previous, so the slab drifts from a clean steel rectangle into a deformed, smeared, scattered residue — Serra's process made visible as its own outcome, live (P4), driven by an ordered list of rules the viewer triggers (P5, order). It reads at two depths (P7): a casual visitor sees a mangled grey box they can bully with buttons; a literate one sees that the internet has quietly been Serra's verb list all along, that "to relate to oneself" becomes a page relating actions to its own DOM. The wit (P9) is dry — the slab even says *this is not described, it is done*, and the words are the first thing "to remove" deletes.

It answers Serra's *Verb List Compilation: Actions to Relate to Oneself*, and stands beside the conceptual instruction lineage (Weiner, LeWitt) without repeating it: those works state a condition or draw a figure; this one hands you the verbs and makes you the one who acts, with no undo but the reload.
