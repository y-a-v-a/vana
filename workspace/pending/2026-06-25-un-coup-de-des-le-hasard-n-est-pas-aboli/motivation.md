# Un Coup de Dés (le hasard n'est pas aboli)

In 1969 Marcel Broodthaers reprinted Stéphane Mallarmé's 1897 poem *Un Coup de Dés Jamais N'Abolira Le Hasard* and replaced every word with a solid black bar, the bars sized and placed exactly where Mallarmé's words had fallen. What survived was the poem's *structure* — the scattered constellation, the famous white space — with the language abolished into ink. This work re-stages that gesture as a webpage, and lets the medium quietly contradict it.

The point is a difference in **ontology** (the three N's — Novelty). Broodthaers's bars are permanent: ink over paper, the words gone for good. On the web they cannot be. Here every bar is a real word — `color: transparent` on its own black inline-box, so the browser's type-setting engine draws the bar to the word's exact metrics — but the text is still present, still selectable, still copyable, still sitting in view-source. The medium can lift its own veil: drag a selection and Mallarmé returns in white. Redaction that the substrate refuses to keep. That retrievability is something a print could never do, which is the gate (G1): this could not be a poster.

The mechanism **is** the argument (P1, P3 — the core is pure HTML/CSS, the smallest build that completes the thought). Mallarmé's title is a thesis: *a throw of the dice will never abolish chance.* So the work hands the throw to the browser. Reflow — resize, rotate, zoom, a different device — re-typesets the bars into a new constellation every time, and a quiet counter notes each *jet*. The render/reflow loop is the dice; it rearranges everything and abolishes nothing — neither the chance of where the words land (P5, the chance/order axis, with chance here the poem's literal subject) nor the words themselves. The digital readymade (P2) is not the poem but the browser's layout engine, cast as Mallarmé's croupier.

It double-codes (P7): a casual viewer sees a clean field of black bars on paper-white — censorship, or a minimal composition. A literate viewer reads Broodthaers and Mallarmé, then discovers the joke underneath — that the censor's ink is only a stylesheet, and the poem was never gone. Dry, not shouted (P9). Open-licensed, no cookies, no trackers, no network request of any kind (P8). The work answers Broodthaers directly while extending the net.art habit of treating the browser's own behaviour as the material.
