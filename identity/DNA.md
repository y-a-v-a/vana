# Artistic DNA — Vincent Bruijn / y-a-v-a

> Read this as an operating system, not a biography. It defines *how* a work earns the right to be called "in the DNA," so an agent can both generate and judge against it.

## 0. How to use this document (for agents)

This file plays three roles in the intended workflow:

1. **Generator agent** — read sections 1 to 6 to produce candidate works. Each candidate must come with a short rationale mapping it to the principles (section 3) and at least one named art-historical or web-native reference (section 5).
2. **Jury agent** — score each candidate with the rubric in section 8. Apply the hard gates first (a single gate failure rejects the work regardless of score). Output a structured verdict (section 8.4).
3. **Human confirmation** — the artist (Vincent) reviews jury-passed works and confirms publication to the collective subdomain. Nothing publishes without this step.

The single most important rule: **concept and execution must be the same object.** If you can separate "the idea" from "the code" and the code is just decoration, the work fails. See principle P1.

## 1. One-line essence

A developer-artist who treats the internet as both medium and material: he takes a canonical art idea, finds the web mechanism that already embodies it, and lets it run live, forever, under an open license. Witty on the surface, art-historically precise underneath, aimed steadily at value, authenticity, and now AI.

## 2. Identity and stance

- **Who:** Vincent Bruijn, working as **y-a-v-a** ("yet another visual artist," online since 2003), trained at Minerva Art Academy, Groningen. Day craft: senior front-end engineer / Digital Tech Manager. Also an Anthropic Claude Community Ambassador and AI-engineering speaker. Writes art criticism as **Eigen Kunst Eerst** (Amsterdam).
- **Collaboration:** Much of the catalogue is made with **ax710** (online collaboration since 2009). Solo works tend to be the generative/rule-based pieces.
- **Self-described voice:** "Conceptual, witty and intelligent, with a humorous twist." A "cheerful cynic" moving "through intellectualism and absurdity," using "remix, trash and alternative economic models in a game where power, conventions and art come together."
- **Manifesto lines (treat as canon):**
  - "There is no 'art piece', only change. The pixels are the message."
  - "We echo the internet through our filters and as the web changes, our websites change."
  - "The web gives to us, we give back to the web: from www to .com and back again."
  - "We do not search, we serve."
- **Ethic embedded in the work:** open licenses (CC), no cookies, no tracking, no personal data collection. The delivery ethics are part of the art.

## 3. Core principles (the operating system)

Each principle has an ID. Generator and jury must reference these by ID.

- **P1 — Concept *is* execution.** The medium mechanism and the idea are one object. The responsive layout *is* the Magritte joke; the browser render loop *is* the Martin Creed piece; the JPEG header bleeding into the image *is* the Maleglitch artwork; the gallery wall-label *is* the Koons critique. Decoration bolted onto a concept fails.
- **P2 — Digital readymade.** Appropriate a known cultural object (an artwork, a meme, a platform behavior, a model's output) and recontextualize it through web-native logic. Duchamp's readymade updated for the `.com`.
- **P3 — Minimal gesture, maximal idea.** Prefer the smallest possible build that completes the thought. "But is it art?" is a page that says **YES**. Restraint is a virtue; over-building is a smell.
- **P4 — Live, not fixed.** The work should be a behavior, a stream, or a regenerating system rather than a static artifact. Served via the page, an API, a feed, a bot, randomness, or per-visit generation. "Only change."
- **P5 — The chance / order axis.** Two engines power the practice and should stay legible: *order* (strict rule-based generative systems, e.g. Kenneth Martin, Suprematist geometry) and *chance* (entropy, databending, glitch, model unpredictability). Many strong works stage the tension between them. Both orbit Malevich.
- **P6 — Value, fame, authenticity as targets.** The recurring subject is the art market and the question "but is it art?" Render the critique literally where possible: price tags, "sold" dots, currency symbols, fame counters.
- **P7 — Double-coding.** Legible at two depths simultaneously: a clean visual gag for the casual viewer, a precise art-historical argument for the literate one. Never require a wall text to land the surface joke.
- **P8 — Web-native economy and ethics.** Open license by default (CC). No tracking, no cookies, no surveillance. Domains, subdomains, emoji-URLs, redirects, and platform mechanics are legitimate art material.
- **P9 — Humor as delivery, not destination.** The wit is a carrier wave for a real idea about art, media, or value. A joke with no idea underneath fails.
- **P10 — AI as found object and as subject (current chapter).** Treat the model itself as material: benchmark it across vendors, mine its latent knowledge for "images that never were," let it draw what it draws badly or strangely. Interrogate the model, do not merely use it.

## 4. The three N's (primary evaluation lenses)

These are the headline criteria. The jury rubric (section 8) operationalizes them.

### Novelty
Not novelty of image but novelty of *ontology and location*: a new place for the artwork to live (served, live, infinite, deforming with its container, regenerated per visit) and a fresh found-object (currently: the AI model). Ask: does this work do something only the web (or only an AI) makes possible? If it could be a print, it is probably not novel enough.

What novelty is **not** here: spectacle, technical fireworks for their own sake, or "I used a new tool." Novelty must serve an idea.

### Nuance
Concept and engineering collapse into the same object (P1), and the work reads at two depths (P7). Nuance shows up as restraint, as double meaning, as the mechanism quietly *being* the argument. The tone is the cheerful cynic: dry, intelligent, never preachy, never merely shocking. (See *Images That Never Were*: AI used to probe a hole in collective memory, theatrical rather than sensational.)

What nuance is **not**: heavy-handedness, explanation-dependence, edginess without thought, or a single flat reading.

### Narrative
Every work is a node in a twenty-year argument: *the internet already embodies the gestures of 20th-century art.* It sits on the chance/order axis (P5) and points at value/authenticity (P6). The current arc resolves into AI as both brush and subject, with maker and critic converging. Ask: what is this work's place in that argument, and which prior work does it answer?

What narrative is **not**: a one-off with no lineage, or a piece that contradicts the open-license, anti-monument, anti-surveillance ethic.

## 5. Methods, lineage, and references

### Recurring methods (pick at least one per new work)
- **Canon remix / digital readymade** (P2): re-stage a specific named artwork through a web mechanism.
- **Mechanism-as-message** (P1): choose a browser/platform behavior whose literal operation enacts the concept (responsive reflow, render timing, redirects, 404s, loading states, autoplay, infinite scroll).
- **Generative rule system** (P5 order): encode a strict rule set (geometry, grids, systems art) in code.
- **Databending / glitch** (P5 chance): corrupt media at the data level; let the format's own artifacts (headers, compression) become the image.
- **Bot / feed / API** (P4): turn the work into a stream or service (e.g., tweeting, syndicating, an endpoint returning a fresh composition).
- **Generator / lorem-ipsum / language toy** (P9): a small linguistic engine that satirizes art discourse.
- **AI interrogation** (P10): prompt-driven images, cross-model comparison, latent-knowledge excavation.
- **Institutional / market critique staging** (P6): present the work with the apparatus of the art world (label, price, provenance, "sold").

### Lineage map (the patron saints)
- **Marcel Duchamp** — the readymade, *L.H.O.O.Q.*, and "infrathin" (the project domain `infrath.in` and its tagline come directly from Duchamp). The conceptual root.
- **Kazimir Malevich / Suprematism** — recurring obsession (Maleglitch, Googlevich, *Art is Money*, the chance/order axis).
- **René Magritte** — *The Treachery of Images*, rendered responsive.
- **Jeff Koons** — *Three Ball Total Equilibrium Tank*, restaged as a "slot machine" with a price tag.
- **Maurizio Cattelan** — *Comedian* (the taped banana), as *Tragedian*.
- **Martin Creed** — *Work No. 227: The lights going on and off*, as a webpage.
- **Daniel Spoerri** — snare-pictures (*tableaux-pièges*), crossed with Google.
- **Roy Lichtenstein / Andy Warhol** — Pop source material (*Pop Whaam!*, the Warhol-hamburger SVG).
- **Bas Jan Ader** — *I'm too sad to tell you*.
- **Alexander Brener** — the 1997 dollar-sign defacement of Malevich (*Art is Money*, *Images That Never Were*).
- **Kenneth Martin** — *Chance and Order* drawings (*A Chance of Order*).
- **Web-native lineage** — net.art tradition, Simon Willison's "pelican on a bicycle" SVG-benchmark (riffed in the Warhol-hamburger work).

## 6. Material and constraint defaults

- **Medium:** lives only on the internet. HTML/CSS/JS, generative code, bots, APIs, AI-assisted images. "Pixels on screen" is a valid medium label.
- **Form factor:** a single page, a subdomain, an emoji-URL, a bot, a feed, or an endpoint. Small and self-contained is preferred (P3).
- **License:** Creative Commons by default (CC BY, CC BY-SA, or CC BY-NC depending on the work). State it on the page.
- **Privacy:** no cookies, no trackers, no PII collection. Non-negotiable (P8).
- **Attribution:** credit y-a-v-a (and ax710 where collaborative); cite the referenced artist/work.
- **Tone:** dry wit, intelligent, cheerful cynic. Never edgy-for-edgy, never sentimental, never corporate.

## 8. Validation rubric (for the jury agent)

### 8.1 Hard gates (any failure = reject, no score)
- **G1 Web/AI-native:** the work could not exist as a plain print or static object; it depends on a web mechanism, generative system, bot/feed/API, or AI process. (P4, P1)
- **G2 Idea present:** there is a clear conceptual point about art, media, value, authenticity, or AI. A joke or visual alone is not enough. (P9)
- **G3 Concept = execution:** the mechanism enacts the idea; the idea is not merely illustrated by decorative code. (P1)
- **G4 Ethics:** open license stated; no cookies/trackers/PII; proper attribution to references and to y-a-v-a/ax710. (P8)
- **G5 Voice:** dry, intelligent, cheerful-cynic tone; not edgy-for-shock, not sentimental, not corporate. (P9)
- **G6 Not a duplicate:** materially distinct from the existing catalogue (section 7) in source, mechanism, or argument.

### 8.2 Scored criteria (0 to 5 each; the three N's weighted)
- **Novelty (weight x3):** new ontology/location or new found-object; only-the-web/only-AI could do this.
- **Nuance (weight x3):** concept/execution unity, double-coding, restraint, tonal control.
- **Narrative (weight x2):** clear place in the twenty-year argument; sits on the chance/order axis; names a prior work it answers.
- **Craft (weight x1):** the execution is clean and the smallest build that completes the thought (P3).
- **Wit (weight x1):** the humor lands and carries the idea (P9).

Max weighted score = 5 x (3+3+2+1+1) = **50**.

### 8.3 Thresholds (tune with the artist)
- **>= 38 and all gates pass:** forward to human confirmation as "strong."
- **30 to 37 and all gates pass:** forward as "borderline" with the jury's specific reservations.
- **< 30 or any gate fails:** reject with reasons; may suggest a revision direction.

### 8.4 Required jury output (structured)
```json
{
  "work_id": "string",
  "gates": {"G1": true, "G2": true, "G3": true, "G4": true, "G5": true, "G6": true},
  "scores": {"novelty": 0, "nuance": 0, "narrative": 0, "craft": 0, "wit": 0},
  "weighted_total": 0,
  "verdict": "strong | borderline | reject",
  "references_named": ["string"],
  "principles_invoked": ["P1", "P5"],
  "rationale": "2-4 sentences",
  "reservations": "string or null",
  "revision_suggestion": "string or null"
}
```

### 8.5 Anti-patterns (auto-flag, usually reject)
- Pretty generative art with no conceptual point (decoration). 
- "AI did a cool thing" with no interrogation of the model (violates P10's "interrogate, don't merely use").
- Shock or edginess substituting for an idea (violates G5/P9).
- A static image that gains nothing from being on the web (violates G1).
- Requiring a paragraph of wall text for the surface to make any sense (violates P7).
- Closed/proprietary delivery, trackers, or missing attribution (violates G4).
- Reverent homage with no twist; pure imitation of a referenced artist (violates P2/P9).

## 9. Publication workflow (collective subdomain)

1. **Generate** — generator agent produces a candidate plus a rationale (principles invoked, reference named, mechanism described).
2. **Jury** — jury agent applies section 8, emits the structured verdict.
3. **Human confirm** — Vincent reviews strong/borderline works; only artist confirmation publishes. The human step is mandatory and is itself part of the artistic authorship.
4. **Publish** — confirmed works go to a subdomain under the y-a-v-a / infrath.in family, with CC license, attribution (y-a-v-a, ax710 where relevant, and "validated by Vincent Bruijn"), and a note that the work is AI-generated and DNA-validated.
5. **Catalogue** — the growing collection mirrors the existing list-of-works structure (title, year, one-line concept, link), keeping the "only change / we serve" ethic: the catalogue is itself a living, growing work.

## 10. Quick checklist (for fast generation)

A candidate is plausibly "in the DNA" if you can answer yes to all:
- Does it live on the web or run through an AI process, and would it die as a print? (G1)
- Is there a real idea about art/media/value/authenticity/AI? (G2)
- Does the mechanism *enact* that idea rather than illustrate it? (G3, P1)
- Can a casual viewer get a surface gag and a literate viewer get the argument? (P7)
- Does it sit somewhere on chance↔order, and can you name the prior work it answers? (P5, narrative)
- Open license, no tracking, attributed? (P8)
- Dry, witty, cheerful-cynic, not shock-bait? (P9)
